# Implementation of a 1D Gaussian-modulated CV-QKD under finite-size parameter estimation

## Abstract

> Continuous-variable quantum key distribution (CV-QKD) is a promising technology for quantum-
> secure communications, offering high secret-key rates and straightforward integration with
> coherent optical communication systems. Conventional Gaussian-modulated CV-QKD encodes
> information in both optical-field quadratures, increasing requirements for state preparation,
> modulation, and calibration, while potentially introducing additional preparation imperfections.
> Unidimensional (1D) Gaussian modulation offers a simpler alternative by encoding information in
> only one quadrature, while the conjugate quadrature constrains the protocol's security. However,
> an integrated experimental 1D implementation combining independent-laser coherent reception with
> practical reconciliation, finite-size parameter estimation, and privacy amplification remains
> challenging. Here, we experimentally demonstrate a 1D Gaussian-modulated CV-QKD system over
> 12~km of standard single-mode fiber using a local local oscillator (LLO) architecture with
> independent lasers and an integrated offline classical post-processing chain. The implementation
> includes coherent detection, digital signal processing, information reconciliation, parameter
> estimation, physicality-constrained security analysis, and privacy amplification. The 1D system
> achieves a privacy-amplified rate of $1.421$~Mbit/s under the asymptotic security treatment and
> $0.364$~Mbit/s under the finite-size scenario. These results establish an experimental baseline
> for the performance--complexity trade-off associated with unidimensional Gaussian modulation and
> provide an experimental basis for developing lower-complexity CV-QKD transmitters.
