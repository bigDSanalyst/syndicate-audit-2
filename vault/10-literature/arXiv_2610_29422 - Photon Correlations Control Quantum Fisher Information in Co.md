# Photon Correlations Control Quantum Fisher Information in Coherent Multidimensional Spectroscopy

## Abstract

> Coherent multidimensional spectroscopy probes interactions and dynamics of molecular systems
> through nonlinear optical signals. Quantum light excitation has been explored as a means of
> controlling these signals, raising the question of how excitation-light correlations affect the
> precision of molecular parameter estimation from the emitted signal. In this work, we show that
> photon-number correlations and two-photon coherence play distinct roles in encoding molecular
> information in the emitted rephasing signal. In the weak-emission regime and within the single
> Schmidt pair approximation, we derive a normally ordered information law in which the joint
> photon-number moment and the squared magnitude of the two-photon coherence weight the amplitude
> and phase sensitivities of the molecular response, respectively. For matched temporal modes and
> mean photon numbers in each input mode, two-mode squeezed vacuum enhances both weights relative
> to coherent excitation, whereas independent displaced squeezing preserves the amplitude weight
> but reduces the phase weight. The background population and its parameter dependence also affect
> the total quantum Fisher information, which therefore cannot be inferred from the coherent mean-
> field signal alone. We derive a leading-order bound on its enhancement over coherent excitation
> when the background population is nonnegative and common to the compared sources. These findings
> provide criteria for selecting excitation sources according to how the target molecular
> parameter affects the amplitude and phase of the response.
