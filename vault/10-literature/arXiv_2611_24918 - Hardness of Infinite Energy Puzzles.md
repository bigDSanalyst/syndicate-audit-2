# Hardness of "Infinite Energy" Puzzles

## Abstract

> Infinite Energy (by Light Potato, 2025) is a single-player puzzle in which one wires a network
> of sockets so that, after every power source is unplugged, energy keeps circulating and leaves
> every board energized. We study several variants of the game and the complexity of deciding
> whether an instance can be won. The variants can be played at https://infinite-energy-peer-
> review.netlify.app, which also has worked examples of the reduction techniques. The colorless
> game, with connectability given by an arbitrary matrix, is NP-hard (by reduction from
> Hamiltonian Path) and lies in PSPACE. We give a seven-socket instance winnable only by charging
> a temporary battery loop and then breaking it, so the natural "charge and keep" strategy fails.
> We reduce NP-membership to one conjecture: that every winnable instance has a winning play in
> which no socket is charged twice. Two variants bound the complexity more tightly. The
> unconstrained variant, in which connectability is unrestricted, is $\mathsf{TC}^0$-complete
> under $\mathsf{AC}^0$ Turing reductions (hence in L). The geometric variant, in which
> connectability is set by proximity of points in $\mathbb{R}^k$, stays NP-hard for every fixed $k
> \ge 2$ via reduction from Hamiltonian Path over grid graphs. The one-dimensional case is open.
> The colored variant is NP-hard, though a matching upper bound remains open.
