# Electrical Stark Tuning and In Situ Field Sensing of $V_{2}$ Silicon Vacancies in a 4H-SiC a-Plane Lateral pin-Diode

## Abstract

> Silicon carbide has emerged as a highly promising platform for scalable quantum technologies,
> with the silicon vacancy serving as a robust spin qubit and quantum sensor. However,
> conventional c-plane 4H-SiC restricts the experimental scheme and resonant optical excitation to
> the sample's geometry. The usage of a-plane substrates enables more versatile resonant optical
> access to the color center through the wafer surface. Here, we demonstrate the electrical
> control and optical addressability of $V_{2}$ (cubic lattice site) silicon vacancies within the
> intrinsic region of lateral pin-diodes fabricated on a-plane 4H-SiC wafers using a scalable
> complementary metal-oxide-semiconductor (CMOS) process. Despite extensive semiconductor
> processing, the integrated color centers exhibit good optical quality, evidenced by narrow
> optical transition linewidths below 80 MHz at zero bias. These embedded quantum sensors are
> utilized for in situ measurement of local electric fields at cryogenic temperatures via the
> Stark effect. Comparisons with macroscopic technology computer-aided design simulations reveal
> the value of these integrated quantum sensors to help understand cryogenic device behavior, for
> example in predicting highly localized field crowding. Furthermore, to address the spectral
> indistinguishability required for quantum networks, we precisely tune the zero-phonon line (ZPL)
> of individual emitters via the reverse-bias Stark effect. This successfully compensates for the
> spectral variations of distinct emitters. Following the relaxation of transient charge
> fluctuations, the Stark-tuned emitters exhibit spectral stability over measurement periods
> exceeding four hours. Our results establish a-plane 4H-SiC as a robust platform for future
> interference experiments and quantum photonic integrated circuits.
