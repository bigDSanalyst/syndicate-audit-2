# Structured light for addressable two-qubit gates in medium-sized ion crystals: modeling motional and dephasing errors

## Abstract

> Geometric phase gates with addressed laser radiation are widely used in trapped-ion quantum
> computers to drive high-fidelity entangling gates on an arbitrary qubit pair of a larger ion
> chain. Such programmable connectivity exploits the collective phonons to mediate the desired
> interaction, while simultaneously achieving robustness to their thermal fluctuations by a
> careful calibration of the gate time with respect to the laser and vibrational-mode frequencies.
> Crosstalk errors can arise when the spectator qubits, which should ideally remain idle during
> the gate, are partially driven by the laser fields. The minimal crosstalk, which is set by the
> laser beam size on the spectator ions, can be achieved when the addressing beams propagate
> perpendicular to the ion chain. However, this results in exciting the radial phonons, and can
> complicate achieving high-fidelities as the ion chain grows due to the so-called spectral mode
> crowding. In this work, we present the theoretical description of a scheme recently implemented
> by Faorlin et $al.$. This scheme overcomes these complications by engineering addressable state-
> dependent forces along the ion-chain with laser beams that propagate perpendicular to it through
> the use of structured light gradients. In this way, one can excite the axial phonons and avoid
> the spectral mode crowding, while simultaneously minimizing the crosstalk. We present a detailed
> derivation of the entangling gate scheme, together with analytical closed-form expressions for
> the leading contribution to the gate error caused by thermal motional fluctuations and dephasing
> noise. Our results confirm that such fluctuations account for up to 93$\%$ of the error budget
> under the operating conditions of the gate implemented by Faorlin et $al.$. We identify target
> parameters to reduce the motional error from the reported $10^{-3}$ to the $10^{-5}$ level.
