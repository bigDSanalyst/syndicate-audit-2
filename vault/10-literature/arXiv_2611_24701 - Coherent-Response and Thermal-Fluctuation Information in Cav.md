# Coherent-Response and Thermal-Fluctuation Information in Cavity-Magnon Magnetometry

## Abstract

> Magnetic sensing with cavity--magnon systems typically relies on the coherent response, while
> field-dependent fluctuations are treated as measurement noise. Here, we show that the variance
> of a measured microwave quadrature provides a distinct source of magnetic-field information. For
> repeated phase-sensitive measurements of a calibrated quadrature, we derive the Gaussian
> likelihood and show that its Fisher information separates exactly into two terms. The coherent-
> displacement term depends on the field derivative of the quadrature mean, and the thermal-
> fluctuation term depends on the field derivative of its variance. Both terms are accessible from
> the same measurement record, but their optimal operating conditions differ. For the amplitude
> quadrature, the dimensionless coherent response factor reaches its global maximum at
> cooperativity $C=2$, corresponding to a coupling strength $g=\sqrt{2κ_aκ_m}$, and at linewidth-
> normalized detunings $δ_a=δ_m=\pm 1$. In the near-resonance approximation, the reduced variance-
> information term is globally maximized at cavity--magnon frequency matching, $Δ_a=Δ_m$, and
> approaches a finite limit at large cooperativity. We map the relative contributions of the two
> terms across drive frequencies, drive strengths, and temperatures. Strong coherent driving
> favors displacement-based sensing, whereas at finite temperature the variance contribution can
> dominate under weak driving and remain finite even when the coherent-displacement contribution
> vanishes. These results establish a measurement-specific framework for cavity--magnon
> magnetometry and identify operating regimes for exploiting the quadrature mean, its variance, or
> both.
