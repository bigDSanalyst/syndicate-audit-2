# The Neptunian ridge through the lens of TESS

## Abstract

> The Neptunian desert and savanna are separated by a ridge, an overdensity at orbital periods of
> approximately 3-6 d identified with Kepler. Like the hot Jupiter pileup at larger radii, this
> feature has been linked to the accumulation of successful outcomes of high-eccentricity
> migration (HEM). We revisit the ridge independently using TESS occurrence posteriors for FGK
> main-sequence stars. We quantify its excess over adjacent regions and compare six occurrence
> profiles using leave-one-out cross-validation and Bayesian evidence. For planets with radii of
> approximately 5-8 $R_\oplus$, the ridge has twice the occurrence density of the denser
> neighbouring region ($J=2.05^{+0.52}_{-0.44}$), with 99.9% posterior support (one-sided Gaussian
> equivalent of $3.1σ$). This contrast agrees with the discovery study and earlier Kepler
> occurrence measurements. Both predictive performance and Bayesian evidence strongly favour
> profiles peaking within the ridge over a single power law. The lognormal is preferred,
> suggesting a narrower inner wing and a more extended outer wing, although evidence for asymmetry
> remains weak. In the same period window, the analysis recovers the hot Jupiter pileup
> (approximately 10-16 $R_\oplus$) with a contrast consistent with that of the ridge and excludes
> an analogous excess among sub-Neptunes (approximately 2-4 $R_\oplus$) with $3.4σ$ posterior
> support. TESS thus provides an independent detection of the Neptunian ridge. Its possible
> asymmetry would be compatible with HEM, in which tidal disruption could produce a sharper inner
> edge and variations in circularisation efficiency a more gradual outer decline. Establishing
> whether the ridge has this shape requires more precise occurrence measurements, with PLATO's
> expected planet yield offering a promising opportunity to help unveil its morphology.
