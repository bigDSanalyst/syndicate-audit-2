# Can machines reason non-verbally?

## Abstract

> Modern machines can solve equations, follow logic and reason through language, yet they still
> struggle with problems that require visual non-verbal reasoning. This is evident even in
> advanced systems such as DeepSeek, ChatGPT and Gemini, which remain limited on visual tasks such
> as completing abstract patterns or delineating ambiguous regions-of-interest. Unlike explicit
> intermediate steps in verbal reasoning which can be verified through logic or automated tests,
> visual reasoning involves implicit intermediate steps with no clear methods for verification.
> Non-verbal reasoning is therefore challenging to model and, as a result, remains a fundamental
> limitation of current machine intelligence. Here we introduce GenVerOpt, a general framework
> that unifies generation, verification and optimisation into a reasoning pipeline for machines.
> By generating candidate solutions, verifying their correctness, and iteratively refining them,
> GenVerOpt enables per-sample optimisation rather than relying solely on dataset-level trends
> captured during model training. This process mirrors human problem-solving where multiple
> possibilities are imagined, evaluated, and refined until a correct solution emerges. Across
> diverse domains, GenVerOpt demonstrates capabilities beyond existing systems: it surpasses
> humans on abstract IQ puzzles, matches clinicians in prostate cancer segmentation, exceeds
> experts in liver vessel reconstruction and outperforms state-of-the-art approaches in tumour
> boundary alignment. These results show that machine reasoning can extend beyond language,
> establishing visual non-verbal reasoning as a foundation for more flexible and generalisable
> intelligence.
