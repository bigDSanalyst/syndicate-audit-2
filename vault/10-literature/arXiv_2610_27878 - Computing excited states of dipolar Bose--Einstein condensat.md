# Computing excited states of dipolar Bose--Einstein condensates by constrained gentlest ascent dynamics

## Abstract

> Dipolar Bose--Einstein condensates (BECs), i.e., BECs with long-range dipole-dipole interactions
> (DDI), exhibit extraordinarily rich physics and serve as a versatile experimental platform for
> exploring exotic quantum states of matter. Within the mean-field framework, a dipolar BEC can be
> described by the Gross--Pitaevskii (GP) model with a DDI term. Its stationary states consist of
> the ground state, which minimizes the GP energy functional under the normalization constraint,
> and excited states, which are higher-energy critical points that typically correspond to
> constrained saddle points. Excited states encode collective excitation modes and play a key role
> in the dynamical stability and quantum phase transitions of cold-atom systems. However, in
> contrast to the extensively studied ground state, they remain far less explored numerically,
> especially in dipolar systems. In this work, we develop a numerical method based on the
> constrained gentlest ascent dynamics (CGAD) that systematically computes excited states of
> dipolar BECs by stably targeting constrained saddle points with any prescribed Morse index.
> Specifically, a semi-implicit sine-pseudospectral discretization with an orthonormalization
> correction step is adopted, and the nonlocal DDI terms are evaluated efficiently and accurately
> via the nonuniform fast Fourier transform (NUFFT). The resulting scheme is applied to one-,
> two-, and three-dimensional dipolar BECs, enabling a detailed numerical investigation of their
> excited states. Remarkably, our simulations reveal that excited states can persist even in
> regimes where the ground state of the condensate becomes mean-field unstable. This work provides
> a computational framework for studying excited states in dipolar quantum gases and offers
> insights for future theoretical and experimental investigations.
