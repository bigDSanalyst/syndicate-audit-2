# Critical droplets and metastability in long-range chains

## Abstract

> Metastable decay in first-order phase transitions proceeds through the nucleation of critical
> droplets. In the one-dimensional nearest-neighbor Ising chain, the cost of the two walls
> bounding a droplet is independent of its size. For interactions decaying as $1/r^{1+σ}$, this
> surface cost instead depends on the droplet size and competes with the bulk free-energy gain.
> The critical radius scales as $R_c\propto h^{-1/σ}$ at small $h$, where $h>0$ is the bias field.
> The value $σ=1$ separates two regimes: for $σ<1$, the activation barrier diverges as $h\to0^+$;
> at $σ=1$, it grows logarithmically; and for $σ>1$, it remains finite, as in the nearest-neighbor
> chain. A nonlocal $φ^4$ field theory yields critical droplets with algebraic tails and confirms
> the predicted radius and barrier scalings, while stochastic long-range dynamics independently
> reproduces the critical-radius scaling.
