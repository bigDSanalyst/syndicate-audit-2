# Postselection on Halting and Reductions to the Halting Problem

## Abstract

> A probabilistic Turing machine is an ordinary Turing machine that may also toss fair coins, with
> no limit on its running time or memory. If it always halts, it decides only computable problems.
> Aaronson et al. studied machines that may run forever on some coin tosses, with the answer read
> only from the runs that halt. Suppose the correct answer must be more than twice as likely as
> the wrong one. They showed that these machines decide exactly the problems solvable with a fixed
> list of questions about the halting problem. They asked what happens when the correct answer
> need only be the more likely one. We show that the machines then decide exactly the problems
> solvable by asking such questions one after another, each chosen after the earlier answers. We
> also show that the factor two can be replaced by any fixed factor greater than one. A machine
> may promise two numbers: a lower bound on its probability of halting, and a lower bound on its
> probability of being right when it halts. From these two numbers we compute exactly how many
> questions fixed in advance the machine can replace. The main idea is that probability acts as a
> budget for how often a computable guess may change its mind. The results extend to oracle
> machines and to machines nested inside each other. Quantum machines decide the same problems.
