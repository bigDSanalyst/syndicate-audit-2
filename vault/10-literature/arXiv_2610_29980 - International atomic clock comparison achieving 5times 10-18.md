# International atomic clock comparison achieving $5\times 10^{-18}$ uncertainty

## Abstract

> Two single-ion optical clocks, located in the United Kingdom and in Germany, are compared
> remotely via a 2220\,km long optical fiber link. Both clocks are based on the ${}^2S_{1/2}
> (F=0)\leftrightarrow {}^2F_{7/2} (F=3)$ electric octupole (E3) transition in
> $^{171}\text{Yb}^+$. Using more than $10^6$ seconds of measurement data, we determine a
> fractional frequency difference of $1.0(4.8)\times 10^{-18}$, demonstrating agreement of the
> independently developed systems. To our knowledge, this constitutes the first international
> clock comparison reaching a fractional uncertainty below $5\times 10^{-18}$, an important
> milestone towards an anticipated optical redefinition of the SI second. The measurement
> uncertainty is dominated by that of the relativistic redshift correction between the two sites,
> which includes time-varying gravity potential differences. We exploit the large baseline between
> the two clocks to search for violations of Lorentz invariance. In the absence of statistically
> significant modulations, we improve constraints on the Robertson-Mansouri-Sexl parameter $α$ by
> approximately a factor of five in the uncertainty, and provide limits on corresponding
> parameters within the Standard-Model Extension framework.
