# Probing gravity at megahertz frequencies with levitated rotors

## Abstract

> The near-field gravitational interaction between masses is always thought of as instantaneous,
> an assumption experimentally tested only up to 1.7 kHz, where measurements yield a bounds at the
> $10^{-5}$ s level. We propose a near-field experiment reaching up to megahertz frequencies, in
> which a levitated microrotor modulates a mass quadrupole and a second rotor measures the
> resulting gravitational tidal field as a torque. Because the detector rotor acts as a mechanical
> local oscillator, the megahertz signal is downconverted to a convenient band of a few hertz, and
> since at fixed geometry neither the torque nor the integration time depends on the rotation
> rate, higher frequencies are reached at no cost in sensitivity. With parameters within reach of
> magnetically levitated NdFeB rotors, a signal at 2 MHz yields a torque of $2\cdot 10^{-25}$ Nm,
> detectable in under an hour at 10 mK. Treating a possible departure from instantaneous
> gravitational coupling through a field relaxation time, the measured torque could constrain it
> at the $10^{-8}$ s level, improving on the existing bounds by more than three orders of
> magnitude. As a byproduct of our analysis, we derive the sensitivity of a rotor operated as a
> tunable high-frequency gravitational-wave detector, finding that a noise-equivalent strain
> around $10^{-22}$ Hz$^{-1/2}$ could be achievable in the megahertz band.
