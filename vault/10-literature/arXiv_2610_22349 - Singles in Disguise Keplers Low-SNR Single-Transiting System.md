# Singles in Disguise: Kepler's Low-SNR Single-Transiting Systems as Incompletely Detected Multis

## Abstract

> NASA's Kepler mission found more systems with one transiting planet (``singles'') than expected
> from those with several (``multis''). This excess was first taken as evidence for a Kepler
> dichotomy -- a second population of intrinsically single or highly inclined systems -- but later
> work argued that selection effects acting on a single population can reproduce it. Singles also
> show a puzzling excess of low signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) detections relative to multis. The
> question arises whether this excess reflects false positives, a selection effect, or an inherent
> population difference. Here we show, using the ExoMOD population synthesis model and the Kepler
> DR25 catalog, that multis drawn from the same parent population as singles have shorter mean
> orbital periods, and thus higher-SNR detections on average. Detecting several planets in one
> system favors compact architectures, so long-period, low-SNR detections are preferentially
> cataloged as singles, most of them intrinsically multi-planet systems whose other planets went
> undetected. The low-SNR excess of singles therefore requires neither false positives nor a
> Kepler dichotomy but rather only the geometry and period sensitivity of transit detection.
