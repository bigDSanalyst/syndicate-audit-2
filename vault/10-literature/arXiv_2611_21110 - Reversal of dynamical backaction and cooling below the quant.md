# Reversal of dynamical backaction and cooling below the quantum backaction limit in Floquet-driven multimode optomechanical resonators

## Abstract

> Dynamical backaction describes how a driven cavity field responds with delay to mechanical
> motion, and can be used to cool that motion toward its quantum ground state or amplify it into
> self-oscillation. Control over this interaction underpins laser cooling, high-fidelity state
> preparation, quantum transduction, and phonon lasing. In conventional cavity optomechanics, both
> the sign and the magnitude of the optical damping are dictated by the laser detuning, and
> cooling is bounded by quantum backaction (QBA), the optomechanical analogue of the Doppler limit
> in cold atoms. We show, in theory and experiment, that dynamical backaction can be strongly
> enhanced in multimode optomechanical resonators driven by time-varying (Floquet) optical fields.
> A modulated drive couples a sideband-unresolved MHz mode to a sideband-resolved GHz mode through
> the shared cavity, replacing the QBA limit of the target mode by the occupancy of the auxiliary
> mode. Experimentally, we cool the MHz mode to $47 \pm 13$ phonons, about half its single-mode
> QBA limit and an order of magnitude below what single-tone backaction reaches at the same
> intracavity photon number. The character of the interaction can moreover be completely inverted
> at fixed laser detuning, turning cooling into amplification and vice versa, controlled solely by
> the modulation frequency. Floquet driving thus extends open-loop quantum control, which requires
> no measurement or feedback, to mechanical resonators outside the resolved-sideband regime.
