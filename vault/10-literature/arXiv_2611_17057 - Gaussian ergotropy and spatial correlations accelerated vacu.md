# Gaussian ergotropy and spatial correlations: accelerated vacuum versus a thermal field

## Abstract

> Can the spatial correlations of a quantum environment be revealed through work extraction? We
> address this question using two harmonic-oscillator detectors coupled to either an accelerated
> vacuum or a matched inertial thermal field. We establish a quantitative connection between the
> spatial correlations of the environment and the equilibrium Gaussian ergotropy, showing that the
> leading extractable work is governed by a response-weighted spatial correlation. Although the
> two environments have identical local spectra, their different spatial correlations give rise to
> distinct work responses. We further identify a finite-distance blind point at which a single
> measurement configuration fails to distinguish the two environments. By reversing the direct
> coupling between the detectors, we develop a two-setting scheme that removes this ambiguity and
> reconstructs the underlying spatial correlations from work measurements. This reconstruction
> remains effective beyond the weak-coupling regime. We also show that spatial correlations can
> provide additional global work even when the detector pair is separable, demonstrating that
> entanglement is not a necessary resource for this advantage. Finally, we investigate the
> transient evolution of Gaussian ergotropy and reveal its oscillatory decay and dependence on
> acceleration and detector parameters. Our results establish Gaussian ergotropy as an operational
> probe of spatial correlations in bosonic environments.
