# Equilibrium Statistics and Stability in Random Two-Population and Three-Population Evolutionary Games

## Abstract

> Multi-population matrix evolutionary games provide a framework for studying adaptation and
> strategic interactions among heterogeneous agents. When payoffs are uncertain, however,
> analysing a specified game does not reveal how frequently different evolutionary outcomes occur
> or how their probabilities depend on modelling assumptions. We study two- and three-population
> games with two strategies per population and both within- and between-population pairwise
> interactions, with payoffs fixed during each evolutionary trajectory. For two populations, we
> derive equilibrium-existence probabilities and analytical stability results for generic games
> and three structured classes: diversity, exclusive, and avoidance games. For generic two-
> population games, independent Gaussian sampling of payoff entries and of replicator coefficients
> produces different equilibrium statistics. Interaction structure further distinguishes
> coexistence from attraction: diversity and exclusive games share the same interior-existence
> probability, but only exclusive games admit asymptotically stable interior equilibria. Avoidance
> games admit transversely attracting equilibrium families without locally asymptotically stable
> individual points. For generic three-population games under independent payoff sampling, we
> obtain existence probabilities for all equilibrium types and joint existence-and-stability
> probabilities for boundary equilibria. Monte Carlo simulations support the analytical
> predictions and quantify multistability, while phase portraits illustrate initial-condition
> dependence. Under the same Gaussian payoff-sampling protocol, simulations show a lower frequency
> of fully mixed equilibria but a higher frequency of multistability for three populations than
> for two.
