# Observation and control of polariton noise in a thermal Rydberg atom electrometer

## Abstract

> The establishment of metrics and control of fundamental noise sources in quantum sensing is a
> long-standing goal in quantum science and technology. We report on the observation of polariton-
> mediated optical noise in a thermal Rydberg atom vapor and demonstrate its active control and
> suppression using a radio-frequency field in Rydberg electrometry. The noise exhibits structured
> amplification and reduction across the Rydberg electromagnetically induced transparency
> resonance. This reveals a nonlinear slow-light feedback mechanism in which the polariton medium
> stores and releases probe-field energy. We show that the reference radio-frequency field used in
> Rydberg heterodyne receivers can also tune the medium into an operating regime where high
> electromagnetic-field responsivity coincides with reduced optical noise. This operating-point
> control enables optimization of the electric-field sensitivity within the constraints imposed by
> Rydberg-polariton physics. These results establish polariton noise as a previously unrecognized
> intrinsic noise source in thermal Rydberg vapors and the corresponding control method as a
> crucial aspect of continued efforts to achieve fundamental sensitivity limits in Rydberg
> electrometry.
