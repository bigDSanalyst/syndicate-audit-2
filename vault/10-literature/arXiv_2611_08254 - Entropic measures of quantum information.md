# Entropic measures of quantum information

## Abstract

> Entropy has played a pivotal role in physics ever since its inception by Clausius in the early
> industrial age. Shannon ushered the concept into the information age by using it to describe
> fundamental limits of information processing, founding the field of information theory. Today, a
> fruitful interaction between information theory and physics is pushing our understanding of
> entropy forward. It has revealed, in particular, that no single notion of entropy suffices to
> capture all relevant properties of physical systems, making it necessary to consider a variety
> of entropic measures of quantum information, each with distinct features and uses. This review
> surveys these recent advances in a structured way, covering a comprehensive array of information
> measures, including Rényi and smooth entropies, together with their interrelations. Starting
> with a systematic characterization of entropies that motivates their definitions from natural
> physical principles, we discuss their applications ranging from quantum communication and
> cryptography to resource theories, thermodynamics, and many-body physics.
