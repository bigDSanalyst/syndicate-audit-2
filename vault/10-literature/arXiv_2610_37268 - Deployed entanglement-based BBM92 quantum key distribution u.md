# Deployed entanglement-based BBM92 quantum key distribution using frequency-converted photons emitted by a quantum dot

## Abstract

> Establishing secure communication is an important requirement in existing and future networks.
> Although current solutions rely on classical approaches, quantum mechanics offers the
> possibility of moving from computational to information-theoretic security. Quantum key
> distribution (QKD) has been extensively studied as a means of achieving such security. Recently,
> epitaxial quantum dots started to be studied and employed in quantum cryptography, because of
> their rapid development that made them one of the brightest sources of quantum light to date. In
> particular, they are known to be excellent sources of polarization-entangled photon pairs,
> especially droplet-etched dots operating at $\sim$780 nm. Here, we employ such a quantum dot to
> generate entangled photon pairs under 1 GHz excitation rate. Using quantum frequency conversion,
> we interface the emitted photons with the telecommunication wavelength range while preserving
> their polarization entanglement. This enables quantum key distribution over a 1.59 km field-
> deployed fibre link across the University of Stuttgart - campus Vaihingen, using the BBM92
> protocol: we achieve a secure key rate of 206 Hz over 19 h of stable operation, resulting in a
> total secure key length of 14.2 Mbit. This key enables the safe encryption and decryption of a
> colour image with further implementation of error correction and privacy amplification. These
> results demonstrate important steps towards hybrid quantum networks by combining high-quality
> entangled-photon sources operated at high excitation rates, efficient frequency conversion
> module, and picosecond-level time synchronization in a realistic deployed network.
