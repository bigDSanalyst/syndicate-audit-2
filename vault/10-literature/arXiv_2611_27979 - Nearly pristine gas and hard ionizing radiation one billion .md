# Nearly pristine gas and hard ionizing radiation one billion years after the Big Bang

## Abstract

> The first stars formed out of light elements (H, He, and Li leftover from Big Bang
> Nucleosynthesis) and began the chemical enrichment of the Universe. Identifying this first-
> generation population of stars, known as Population III, and understanding their properties is
> one of the most ambitious goals of modern extragalactic astrophysics. We report the detection of
> He and H emission lines and extremely low Oxygen abundance in AMORE6, a strongly magnified
> galaxy at redshift z = 5.725, i.e. approximately one billion years after the Big Bang, with deep
> James Webb Space Telescope spectroscopy. He II lambda1640 and lambda4686 emission lines are
> detected at 16.7 sigma and 4.0 sigma significance, respectively. The observed He II-to-Hbeta
> ratios are consistent with predictions for extreme ionizing radiation fields and the low
> chemical compositions typical of Population III stars. Oxygen abundance of the interstellar
> medium is estimated to be 8 x 10^-4 solar from weak [OIII]lambda4363 and lambda5008 lines,
> consistent with an almost pristine environment, perhaps enriched by the Pop III stars
> themselves. Although we cannot exclude alternative sources of hard ionizing radiation, such as
> accreting black holes, these observations offer the first view of the extremely low metallicity
> environment where the Universe's first stars are predicted to be born. The data show that such
> pockets of pristine gas still exist one billion years after the Big Bang and that they are
> dominated by very massive stars.
