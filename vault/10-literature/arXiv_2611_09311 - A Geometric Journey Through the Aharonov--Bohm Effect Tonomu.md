# A Geometric Journey Through the Aharonov--Bohm Effect: Tonomura's Toroid, Pseudoscalar Phase, and Quantum Holonomy in Clifford Algebra

## Abstract

> The magnetic Aharonov--Bohm (AB) effect is often introduced through the idealised infinite
> solenoid and a complex scalar phase. We give a geometric-algebra formulation based on the
> compact toroidal magnets shielded by a superconductor employed in the electron-holography
> experiments of Tonomura and collaborators. Working in the real Euclidean Clifford algebra $
> \mathrm{Cl}(3,0)$, we distinguish the unit volume pseudoscalar $I$ (grade 3), coordinate angles
> (scalars, grade 0), tangent vectors (grade 1), and directed surface bivectors (grade 2). The
> magnetic field is represented by the dual bivector $\mathcal{B}= I \mathbf B$, while the
> electromagnetic $U(1)$ phase is represented by the central unit multivector $e^{Iα}$ generated
> by the pseudoscalar $I$, rather than by a spatial rotor with a bivector generator. We establish
> the linked toroidal geometry, formulate magnetic flux and Stokes' theorem intrinsically in
> geometric algebra, and derive the holonomy element $U_Γ=\exp(IqΦ_B/\hbar)$ from $U(1)$ gauge
> covariance, with every sign fixed explicitly. We then express superconducting fluxoid
> quantization in the same notation and obtain the binary electron holonomy $U_Γ= (-1)^N$ in the
> ideal limit where the linked magnetic flux equals $N h/(2e)$. Finally, a short conditional-state
> description of the quantum source and field shows how a backreaction can introduce an additional
> phase and reduce interference visibility. The conditional-state overlap is represented by a
> central scalar--pseudoscalar element; preservation of the fluxoid sector alone does not
> guarantee unit coherence.
