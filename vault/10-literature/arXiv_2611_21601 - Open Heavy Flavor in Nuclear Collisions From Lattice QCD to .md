# Open Heavy Flavor in Nuclear Collisions: From Lattice QCD to Observables

## Abstract

> Heavy quarks serve as excellent probes of the Quark-Gluon-Plasma (QGP) produced in ultra-
> relativistic heavy-ion collisions. Here, we report on a comprehensive framework that transports
> charm quarks from their initial production through the QGP medium, hadronization, and the
> hadronic phase to final observables in experiments. For the transport through the QGP fireball,
> which is described by 2+1D relativistic viscous fluid dynamics, we employ an extended Langevin
> equation that combines elastic diffusion processes with medium-induced radiation, including
> interference effects. The charm-quark transport coefficients are derived from nonperturbative
> $T$-matrix interactions, which are constrained by lattice QCD and also produce an equation of
> state for the thermal medium which is consistent with the one employed in the hydrodynamic
> simulations. Pre-hadronic resonant states which emerge in the $T$-matrix near the QGP transition
> temperature naturally lead to a transition to hadronic degrees of freedom. This transition is
> realized in two different approaches: a coalescence model augmented with 4-momentum conservation
> and the resonance recombination model, both supplemented with fragmentation functions
> constrained by charm-hadron spectra in proton-proton collisions at the LHC and RHIC. The late-
> stage rescattering of charm hadrons is assessed with schematic heavy-light cross sections
> implemented into a hadronic transport model. We report results for the nuclear modification
> factors and elliptic flows of $D^0$ and $D_s^+$ mesons and $Λ_c^+$ baryons, together with the
> $D_s^+/D^0$ and $Λ_c^+/D^0$ yield ratios. The calculations are compared with measurements from
> the ALICE and CMS collaborations in Pb--Pb collisions at $\sqrt{s_{\NN}}=5$\,TeV and with STAR
> data in Au-Au collisions at $\sqrt{s_{\NN}}=200$\,GeV.
