# Multi-phase Carbon Structure and Dynamics in NGC 6334: Insights from CO, [C I], and [C II]

## Abstract

> We present high-angular and velocity resolution observations of the emission lines of [CII] at
> 158 micron, [CI] at 609 micron, and the 12CO and 13CO (J=3-2) transitions toward the central
> region of NGC 6334, providing a detailed multi-phase view of carbon in this massive star-forming
> complex. By combining morphology and spatial scales information, kinematics, line profiles, and
> line ratio analyses, we quantify the distribution and physical state of ionized and atomic
> carbon, and carbon in its molecular form in CO. [CII] emission is the most extended, permeates
> the mapped region and is dynamically complex, while 13CO is compact and quiescent, and 12CO and
> [CI] occupy intermediate regimes connecting dense regions to transitional diffuse layers. We
> interpret these emission distributions such that the brightest [CII] emission traces
> photodissociation regions (PDRs) close to the H II region bubbles located along the central
> filament, while the extended [CII] emission arises from the more diffuse warm gas, indicating
> either cloud formation or dispersal processes. Remarkably, the [CI] is more extended than the
> 13CO, but less than [CII] and may probe the gas velocity structure resulting from colliding
> flows or cloud-cloud collisions. Line ratios involving [CII], such as [CII]/[CI] or [CII]/CO are
> systematically high, revealing widespread diffuse emission and localized UV-illuminated
> surfaces. Line ratio analysis and K-means clustering of [CI]/13CO versus dust continuum (cold
> 870 micron and warm 70 micron) emission reveal stratified chemical structure that UV photons
> penetrate deeply into the clumpy ridge (Av~18), suggesting a porous PDR. These results together
> depict that NGC 6334 is a clumpy, feedback-dominated PDR ridge with coexisting carbon phases,
> where [CI] and [CII] also trace dynamical processing through multiple velocity components linked
> to cloud-cloud collisions.
