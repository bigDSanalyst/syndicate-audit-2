# Sandpile avalanches in two dimensions are finite

## Abstract

> In the abelian sandpile model on $\mathbb{Z}^d$, a site with at least $2d$ grains topples by
> sending one grain to each neighbor. In a box $[-L,L]^d$ whose boundary absorbs grains, adding
> grains at random sites and toppling defines a Markov chain, and Athreya and Járai (2004)
> constructed the stationary state on $\mathbb{Z}^d$ as the limit of its stationary distributions
> as $L\to\infty$. By a formula of Dhar (1990), when a grain is added at the origin of a sandpile
> in the stationary state, the expected number of topplings at a site in the resulting avalanche
> is proportional to the Green function of simple random walk from the origin. This Green function
> is finite for $d\geq3$, where Járai and Redig (2008) proved that avalanches are almost surely
> finite, and infinite for $d=2$. We prove that for $d=2$ avalanches are nevertheless almost
> surely finite: the probability that an avalanche reaches distance $R$ is at most a negative
> power of $\log R$. The proof follows the avalanche backward and reduces the question to an
> estimate for the uniform spanning tree, which we prove by a multiscale electrical argument.
