# Half-Completeness from Standard Gravitational Dispersion Relations

## Abstract

> The completeness hypothesis states that quantum gravity realizes every representation allowed by
> its gauge group. Previous S-matrix arguments used the graviton pole to constrain exchanged
> representations, including a proof of completeness by imposing an additional high-energy bound
> on crossing-odd amplitudes. We investigate what follows without this assumption, using standard
> twice-subtracted dispersion relations, positivity, and crossing symmetry. For external massless
> SU(2) isospin $J$, we prove ``half-completeness'': either massive exchanges include every
> isospin $j\le J$, or at least one $j>J$ is required. At $J=1$, we construct an explicit
> amplitude with only $j=0,1$ exchanges that satisfies these conditions but violates the crossing-
> odd bound. This establishes that the additional high-energy assumption does not follow from the
> standard framework. For $J\ge2$, however, we show that lower-only spectra cannot be realized
> within a class of string-inspired Euler amplitudes, leaving their existence an open question.
> More generally, for scalar matter transforming under any compact internal symmetry group, we
> prove that massive singlets of unbounded Lorentz spin must be exchanged.
