# Exponential mixing time for the Davies dynamics of the quantum random energy model

## Abstract

> We prove exponentially slow thermalization for the quantum random energy model (QREM) under
> Davies dynamics. The QREM is the $n$-qubit Hamiltonian $H=ΓT+U$, where $U$ assigns independent
> Gaussian energies with variance equal to the system size to computational-basis configurations
> and $T$ is the transverse field generated by single-qubit Pauli $X$ operators. Let
> $β_c=\sqrt{2\log 2}$ be the freezing inverse temperature of the classical random energy model.
> For every inverse temperature $β>β_c$ and every transverse-field strength $0\leqΓ<Γ_c(β)$, where
> $e^{ββ_c}=2\cosh(βΓ_c(β))$, we show that the mixing time of the dissipative Davies dynamics
> associated with single-qubit Pauli system--bath couplings grows exponentially with the system
> size, almost surely; the bound is unchanged if the standard zero-Bohr-frequency dephasing terms
> are included. The result is uniform over all Davies rates satisfying detailed balance and a
> polynomial upper bound and does not assume primitivity. In the spin-glass phase, the Gibbs
> weight is carried by a sparse family of deep classical configurations that are separated by an
> extensive Hamming distance. A localization theorem for the QREM [Manai, Warzel, Comm. Math.
> Phys. 402, 1259 (2023)] transfers this geometry to the corresponding low-energy quantum
> eigenstates. Consequently, the Gibbs weight of the intermediate spectral sector is exponentially
> small, while single-qubit bath operators have exponentially small matrix elements between the
> two macroscopic sides of the bottleneck. A Davies-specific flux estimate then converts these two
> facts into an exponential lower bound on the mixing time. This gives a rigorous open-system
> dynamical manifestation of the frozen, localized low-energy structure of the QREM throughout its
> equilibrium spin-glass phase below the quantum transition line.
