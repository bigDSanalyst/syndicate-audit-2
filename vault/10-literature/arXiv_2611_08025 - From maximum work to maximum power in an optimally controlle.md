# From maximum work to maximum power in an optimally controlled quantum Ising Otto engine

## Abstract

> In finite-time quantum Otto engines, maximizing power requires balancing the duration of the
> unitary strokes against the losses from nonadiabatic excitations. We address this trade-off in
> an Otto engine with a free-fermion working medium, using quantum optimal control. Having
> determined the single-particle energy scales that maximize ideal work at fixed reservoir
> temperatures, we realize them as the two endpoint Hamiltonians of a transverse-field Ising
> chain, connected by a path that crosses the quantum critical point. Optimal control of the
> unitary strokes yields near-ideal work on a time scale growing linearly with system size,
> instead of quadratically as for linear driving. We derive a time-rescaling relation that maps
> protocols with different energy scales onto a single reference problem and, for complete
> thermalization strokes of fixed duration, use it to build an analytical model of the cycle
> power. The model predicts two competing maximum-power regimes: a slow branch that approaches the
> ideal work, and a fast, size-independent branch that trades work for a shorter cycle and
> dominates above a crossover size, in quantitative agreement with numerical optimal-control
> results. It also identifies power-optimal energy scales that differ from the work-optimal ones
> and further increase the power. Our results provide a predictive approach to jointly optimizing
> the operating conditions and finite-time dynamics of quantum heat engines.
