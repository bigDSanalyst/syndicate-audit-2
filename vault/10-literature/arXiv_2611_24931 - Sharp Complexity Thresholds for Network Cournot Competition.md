# Sharp Complexity Thresholds for Network Cournot Competition

## Abstract

> We determine the approximation complexity of network Cournot equilibrium as a function of demand
> degree and the number of firms sharing a market. For common, nonincreasing concave polynomial
> inverse demands, convex quadratic costs of firms' allocation vectors, and rational total
> capacities, high-precision approximation is polynomial-time solvable for cubic demands,
> regardless of market size. Quartic demands remain tractable when each market has at most three
> firms, and quintic demands remain tractable with at most two. These thresholds are exact:
> approximation at a fixed positive profit-regret tolerance is PPAD-hard for quartic demands with
> four firms per market, quintic demands with three, and sextic demands with two. Each hardness
> result uses one fixed demand curve in every market, a constant number of markets per firm, and
> globally nondecreasing strongly convex quadratic costs. We also give a polynomial-time algorithm
> for arbitrary-degree nonincreasing concave polynomial demands when each firm's convex polynomial
> cost depends only on its total production. The algorithm applies to arbitrary access graphs and
> permits firm-specific inverse demands. Its running time is polynomial in the input size and the
> logarithm of the inverse accuracy. The algorithm assigns one marginal charge to each firm and
> adjusts the charges until each firm's total sales approximate the quantity it would produce at
> that charge.
