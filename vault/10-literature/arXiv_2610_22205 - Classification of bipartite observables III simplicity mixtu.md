# Classification of bipartite observables III: simplicity, mixtures and quantum error correction

## Abstract

> The paradigm of a simple unstable theory in the sense of Shelah is the theory of the random
> graph, the Fraïssé limit of the finite graphs. Quantum error correction has a paradigm of its
> own, and we show that it is the counterpart of the random graph: the Pauli group on countably
> many qubits, taken modulo phases and with commutation as its relation, is the Fraïssé limit of
> the finite alternating spaces over the two-element field, and its form computes the syndromes of
> stabilizer codes. Seen through this correspondence, simplicity theory turns out to be a theory
> of error correction. Clifford encoding circuits implement the homogeneity of the Pauli group,
> forking recognizes the logical errors, and the independence theorem of Kim and Pillay is the
> gluing of syndromes. The two paradigms look alike as first-order structures, and they part
> company only when observables enter. Each defines a bipartite observable, and we prove that,
> once preparations are mixed, both acquire the tree property of the second kind in real-valued
> logic. They reach it by different roads, however, and we show that the probes decide which. The
> probes of the Pauli observable are the Pauli channels; for them the indicator of each syndrome
> sector is the difference of the responses of two uniform mixtures, and actual mixtures already
> witness the tree. The probes of the Rado observable see only marginals; conditions given by
> actual mixtures remain compatible on disjoint blocks, and the tree must be sought among
> idealized preparations.
