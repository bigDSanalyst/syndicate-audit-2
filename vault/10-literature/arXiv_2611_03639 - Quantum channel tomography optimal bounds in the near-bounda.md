# Quantum channel tomography: optimal bounds in the near-boundary regime

## Abstract

> Quantum channel tomography (also known as quantum process tomography) is a fundamental task in
> quantum information and quantum computing, aiming to learn the full description of an unknown
> quantum channel from black-box queries. For channels with input dimension $d_1$, output
> dimension $d_2$, and Kraus rank at most $r$, [CGO+26] identifies the dilation rate
> $τ=rd_2/d_1\geq 1$ as a key parameter governing a Heisenberg-to-classical transition in query
> complexity. While the optimal bounds in the boundary regime ($τ=1$) and the away-from-boundary
> regime ($τ\geq 1+Ω(1)$) are now known, the precise behavior in the near-boundary regime
> ($1<τ<1+o(1)$) has remained unresolved. In this paper, we close this gap, thereby fully settling
> the query complexity of quantum channel tomography. Specifically, we show that the query
> complexity is - $Ω(rd_1d_2/\varepsilon^2)$ for diamond norm error $\varepsilon$, matching the
> upper bound by [MB26, CGO+26], -
> $Θ(d_1^2/\varepsilon+\min\{(τ-1)d_1^2,(τ-1)^2d_1^2d_2^2\}/\varepsilon^2)$ for Choi trace norm
> error $\varepsilon$. Our results reveal two qualitatively different forms of the Heisenberg-to-
> classical transition. For diamond norm tomography, the Heisenberg scaling disappears suddenly as
> soon as $τ$ leaves the boundary regime. In contrast, Choi trace norm tomography exhibits a
> gradual transition from Heisenberg to classical scaling as $τ$ moves from the boundary regime to
> the away-from-boundary regime.
