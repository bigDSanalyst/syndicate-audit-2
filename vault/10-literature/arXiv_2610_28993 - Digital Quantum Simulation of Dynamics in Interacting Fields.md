# Digital Quantum Simulation of Dynamics in Interacting Fields

## Abstract

> Matter under extreme conditions behaves as a non-equilibrium, strongly-interacting quantum
> system. Such systems are in the non-perturbative regime of quantum chromodynamics (QCD), where
> their time evolution is classically intractable. Quantum computing offers a promising
> alternative, but present-day devices are limited in qubit count and circuit depth, so efficient
> formulations and algorithms are key to scaling toward experimentally relevant QCD simulations. A
> scalable framework is established for simulating energy loss and hadronization in
> 1+1-dimensional SU(2) lattice gauge theory, in which a heavy quark traverses a lattice of light
> quarks. Classical lattice QCD methods are translated into physics-informed circuits to prepare
> the interacting ground state. The evolution is run on IBM's quantum processor ibm_pittsburgh,
> where the charge distribution and energy loss are measured. A further study develops the quantum
> simulation of the fully gauge-fixed SU(2) Hamiltonian in a mixed basis, a formulation that is
> expected to remain efficient at all gauge couplings. Digitizing the continuous gauge fields with
> as few as three qubits per plaquette reproduces low-energy observables with $10^{-3}$ precision.
> Scaling to larger systems is limited by resource overhead, which motivates truncations that
> preserve the relevant observables. Sequency Hierarchy Truncation (SeqHT) exploits the hierarchy
> in basis-operator contributions. Demonstrated on the adiabatic preparation of the $λφ^4$ ground
> state on ibm_sherbrooke, SeqHT reduces circuit depth by ~30%, suppressing hardware noise in the
> extracted observables. All simulations in this dissertation are validated on quantum hardware
> against classical benchmarks. These studies advance quantum simulations of real-time QCD
> dynamics.
