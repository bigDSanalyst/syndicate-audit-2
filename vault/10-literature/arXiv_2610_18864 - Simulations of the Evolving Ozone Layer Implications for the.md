# Simulations of the Evolving Ozone Layer: Implications for the Early Earth, Exoplanets, and the Search For Life

## Abstract

> Over 4 billion years, Earth's atmosphere has been transformed from a composition devoid of
> molecular oxygen (O$_2$) to one where O$_2$ is now 21% of our modern atmosphere by volume. With
> high quantities of O$_2$, an ozone (O$_3$) layer can form and partially shield the surface from
> biologically harmful ultraviolet radiation (UV). The evolution of the O$_3$ layer has previously
> been simulated under the modern Sun by independent groups with separate models. Here, we present
> simulations of Earth's atmosphere from four open-source one-dimensional (1D) photochemical
> models and a 3D chemistry-climate model, focussing on O$_3$. We find that the O$_3$ layer
> thickness exhibits a clear deviation between the 1D models and the 3D model due to several
> factors, including treatments of O$_2$ photolysis, chemical networks, H$_2$O microphysics and 3D
> transport. We find that 1D models introduce a bias for unobserved climate states and show how
> these biases may propagate to calculations of biological UV damage and surface habitability. We
> show that when applied to specific exoplanet atmospheres, these discrepancies can become more
> pronounced, propagating through to observational predictions for the next generation of
> telescopes, such as HWO and LIFE, that will search for habitable and inhabited exoplanets.
