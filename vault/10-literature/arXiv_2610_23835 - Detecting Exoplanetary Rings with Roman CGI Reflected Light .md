# Detecting Exoplanetary Rings with Roman CGI Reflected Light Phase Curve Observations

## Abstract

> The Nancy Grace Roman Space Telescope's Coronagraph Instrument (CGI) will provide the first
> opportunity to directly detect mature giant exoplanets in reflected light. However, most known
> radial-velocity planets lie near CGI's sensitivity limit. In contrast, Saturn-like rings around
> these planets may be much more accessible: our models show that rings can outshine their host
> planets by one to two orders of magnitude or more, making them easier to detect than the planets
> themselves. However, because the planet and rings would be unresolved in reflected light,
> unrecognized rings could lead to misinterpretation of the planet's intrinsic brightness and
> physical properties. To disentangle these blended signals, we model reflected-light phase curves
> of ringed and ringless planets. Ring systems can produce a distinctive phase-curve morphology,
> often with four extrema rather than the two expected for unringed planets, enabling robust
> confirmation with multi-epoch CGI observations. We develop an analytical framework for ringed-
> planet phase curves using a modern, self-consistent coordinate system and use it to illustrate
> phase curves for a range of ring configurations. We then simulate observations of $ε$ Eridani b
> (3.5 AU, 1 M$_{Jup}$), a prime initial CGI target, with a Saturn-like ring system. These
> simulations show that such a configuration would be detectable and suggest that rings may expand
> the pool of viable CGI targets to planets previously considered beyond reach. We conclude that
> CGI is poised to provide the first observational constraints on the prevalence of Saturn-like
> rings around giant exoplanets.
