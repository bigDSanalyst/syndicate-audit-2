# Odd radio circle J1555+2726: Observational evidence for shock re-acceleration of fossil plasma

## Abstract

> Context. Odd radio circles (ORCs) are peculiar extragalactic sources recently discovered. They
> are characterised by one or more bright rings extending on scales of hundreds of kiloparsecs,
> and are filled with fainter emission. A massive elliptical galaxy lies at the centre of ORCs.
> While their formation mechanism is debated, a shock is plausibly involved. Aims. We aim to
> constrain the nature of ORC J1555+2726 (ORC4) through the analysis of its morphological and
> spectral properties. Methods. We utilised Low Frequency ARray (LOFAR), upgraded Giant Metrewave
> Radio Telescope (uGMRT), and MeerKAT radio data at 50-1284 MHz to obtain images and spectral
> index maps at high resolution and sensitivity. Results. Both the resolution and observing
> frequency affect the observed morphology. In particular, the brightness contrast between the
> ring and the filling emission becomes progressively smoother from higher to lower frequencies.
> We measured an integrated spectrum of slope $α=1.06\pm 0.03$, a radio power of $P_{\rm 150}=
> (5.4\pm 0.5)\times 10^{25} \; {\rm W \; Hz^{-1}}$ at 150 MHz, and a radial spectral flattening
> down to $α\sim0.5$ at the ring location. However, we inferred a negative (convex) spectral
> curvature and a peculiar spectral shape in radio colour-colour diagrams, which imply spectral
> flattening towards high frequencies. These properties cannot be explained by shock acceleration
> of thermal particles and standard ageing. Conclusions. The observed features suggest that a
> mixture of electrons with different spectra coexist within ORC4 volume, likely associated with
> shock re-acceleration of a fossil relativistic reservoir. The ring emission is plausibly
> dominated by higher-energy electrons re-accelerated at the shock front, while lower-energy re-
> accelerated electrons are more uniformly distributed across the whole source (possibly
> ellipsoidal) volume.
