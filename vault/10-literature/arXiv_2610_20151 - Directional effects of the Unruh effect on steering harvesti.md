# Directional effects of the Unruh effect on steering harvesting

## Abstract

> We investigate directional quantum steering harvesting by two uniformly accelerated non-
> identical Unruh-DeWitt detectors coupled to a massless scalar field in the Minkowski vacuum. We
> show that the Unruh effect does not simply degrade harvested steering, but can reshape its
> directionality in a strongly parameter-dependent manner. At low detector energy scales,
> acceleration predominantly suppresses steering, whereas at higher energy scales, moderate
> acceleration can enhance steering and even activate an initially absent steering direction. Most
> strikingly, within a finite acceleration window, the Unruh effect enhances steering in one
> direction while simultaneously suppressing it in the opposite direction, revealing an
> intrinsically directional influence of relativistic acceleration. Detector non-identicality
> provides an additional control mechanism: a moderate energy-gap mismatch can enhance or activate
> one steering direction while suppressing the reverse one, whereas sufficiently large energy-gap
> mismatch suppresses the harvested steering. Moreover, the maximal steering asymmetry
> approximately marks the transition from two-way to one-way steering. Our results establish
> relativistic acceleration and detector non-identicality as means of controlling the strength and
> directionality of vacuum-harvested quantum correlations.
