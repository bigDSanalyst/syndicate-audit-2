# Nonlinearity-preserving quantum simulation of flows in the early fault-tolerant regime

## Abstract

> Ensemble prediction of nonlinear dynamics propagates a probability density whose linear
> transport equation retains the nonlinear drift, but grid-dependent circuit costs leave end-to-
> end feasibility in early fault-tolerant quantum computing unresolved. We couple polynomial
> reduced-order models fitted by sparse identification of nonlinear dynamics to Walsh-synthesized
> Koopman-von Neumann (KvN) transport and surface-code resource estimation. At fixed model
> dimension and drift degree, per-step transport rotation counts scale polylogarithmically with
> cells per reduced coordinate. Balancing model accuracy against circuit costs makes reduced-model
> polynomial degree a design variable for early fault-tolerant quantum simulation. For a cylinder
> wake approaching saturation, preparation, propagation and sampling to 1% relative root-mean-
> square field error against the radial model are estimated to take 1.62 h on about 560,000
> physical qubits. Estimated three-coordinate wake runtimes span hours to weeks below one million
> qubits. IBM hardware experiments demonstrate nonlinear radial KvN transport for this wake, using
> reference circuits to mitigate noise.
