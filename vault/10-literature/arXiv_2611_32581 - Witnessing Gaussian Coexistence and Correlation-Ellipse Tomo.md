# Witnessing Gaussian Coexistence and Correlation-Ellipse Tomography

## Abstract

> Gaussian light is specified by second-order field moments, yet their hidden correlation geometry
> can demand higher-order intensity measurements. We establish a measurement hierarchy for
> "Gaussian coexistence", i.e., the simultaneous presence of cross covariance and cross
> pseudocovariance. For two zero-mean Gaussian modes with circular marginals, all unperturbed
> intensity moments of second or third order are blind to coexistence. In contrast, a balanced
> fourth-order residual provides a necessary and sufficient witness. Their identities, however,
> remain fundamentally ambiguous: recovering the two correlation strengths does not reveal which
> belongs to the covariance and which to the pseudocovariance. Unexpectedly, adding thermal
> fluctuations resolves this ambiguity: an independent, correlated thermal probe selectively
> modifies the covariance while leaving the pseudocovariance unchanged, allowing the two
> correlation strengths to be individually identified. Moreover, a coherent reference in only one
> arm then makes their relative phase accessible through third-order intensity correlation.
> Together, these measurements reconstruct a correlation ellipse and its Poincaré-sphere
> coordinates, determining the relative channel strengths and the ellipse orientation. The
> framework separates what intensity detection can witness, what thermal probing can identify, and
> what a single phase reference can orient, turning inaccessible Gaussian correlation structure
> into a quantitatively reconstructible geometry.
