# Spectrally enhanced gravitational redshift signals in photonic interferometry

## Abstract

> Quantum mechanics and general relativity are remarkably successful, yet their joint predictions
> remain beyond direct experimental reach. Photonic interferometers operating in a gravitational
> field offer a promising route to test this regime: gravitational redshift differentiates photon
> propagation between vertically separated arms, leading to a predicted phase shift and reduction
> in interference visibility. These effects are minute on laboratory scales, motivating existing
> proposals to consider large interferometer areas to accumulate them geometrically. Indeed, such
> schemes typically require kilometre-scale optical paths to produce appreciable interferometric
> signals. Here, we propose a strategy that replaces geometric accumulation with spectral
> enhancement by coupling the two interferometer arms to optical scatterers based on ultranarrow
> atomic transitions. These transitions serve as local spectral references: gravitational redshift
> changes the photon's detuning from each resonance, producing large differences in reflection
> amplitude and hence substantial visibility loss and interferometric phase shift already for
> centimetre-scale height differences. We quantify the experimental requirements and assess the
> feasibility of implementation with current or near-term technology. Our proposal thus opens a
> route to tabletop optical tests of the interplay between quantum mechanics and general
> relativity.
