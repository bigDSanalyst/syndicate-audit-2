# Geometry of squeezed states. The phase-space metric, relative vacua and cosmology

## Abstract

> Geometry enters quantum theory in two places. The space of quantum states carries a symplectic
> form, which generates unitary evolution, and a metric, which governs uncertainties and
> transition probabilities. The classical phase space acquires a metric of its own when a vacuum
> is chosen: quantizing a linear system amounts to choosing a compatible complex structure,
> equivalently a compatible metric $g$. We take this second metric as the primary object and show
> that it plays, on phase space, the role that the quantum metric plays on the space of states:
> fluctuations of linear observables are $g$-norms of Hamiltonian vector fields, and along
> coherent states $g/2\hbar$ \emph{is} the quantum metric. Squeezed states then have a simple
> description. A squeezed vacuum is the vacuum of another metric, and squeezing is a property of a
> pair of metrics, a relative vacuum, whose invariants are the eigenvalues $e^{\pm2r_i}$ of
> $L=g_0^{-1}g$. We work out the harmonic oscillator in detail, including why a rotated squeezed
> state no longer minimizes the product of the displayed uncertainties. Quadratic evolution
> deforms the metric, and only the part of the generator that is self-adjoint with respect to the
> reference changes the $r_i$, at a rate we give exactly. For fields, a vacuum is unitarily
> implementable relative to a reference exactly when it lies at finite distance from it in the
> space of vacua. For a massless field in de Sitter space we revisit the two canonical charts
> compared by Grain and Vennin: relative to the reference of the standard squeezing formalism the
> Bunch--Davies state has $\sinh r_k=1/(2|kη|)$, and relative to the vacuum that a radiation era
> beginning at time $η$ would select it has $\sinh r_k=1/(2k^2η^2)$. The second comparison
> squeezes twice as fast and is unitarily implementable; the first is not.
