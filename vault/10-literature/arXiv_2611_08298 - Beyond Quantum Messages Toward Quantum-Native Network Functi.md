# Beyond Quantum Messages: Toward Quantum-Native Network Functioning

## Abstract

> The Quantum Internet is commonly envisioned as a network that transports quantum states and
> distributes entanglement as the key resource underpinning distributed quantum applications. This
> view is necessary but incomplete if quantum phenomena are confined to what the network carries
> or manages, while network functionalities remain grounded predominantly in classical
> abstractions. This Perspective advances a broader thesis: quantum phenomena can also participate
> directly in the realization of network functionalities. We first establish the architectural
> baseline by introducing entanglement as a stateful network resource whose operational condition
> is non-locally distributed across remote nodes, evolves through manipulation or consumption, and
> degrades over time through decoherence. Its evolving state must therefore remain visible to the
> functionalities acting on it. We then move beyond resource visibility and use two mechanisms as
> proof points of a broader quantum-native functional design space: entanglement generation during
> propagation through coherent spatial-path superposition, and operational connectivity induced by
> shared entanglement that can depart from the physical graph. These examples show that paths,
> neighbors, or participating nodes may become operationally relevant in ways determined by
> quantum coherence and shared entanglement, rather than by physical connectivity alone. We
> identify Quantum Addressing as a transversal abstraction capable of representing such relations
> while complementing, rather than replacing, classical addressing and signaling. The resulting
> perspective broadens the Quantum Internet from transporting quantum information and distributing
> entanglement to exploiting quantum phenomena in network functioning, opening a coupled frontier
> spanning routing, forwarding, control, and functionality placement.
