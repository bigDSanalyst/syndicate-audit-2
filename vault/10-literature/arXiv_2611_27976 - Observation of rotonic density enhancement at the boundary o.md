# Observation of rotonic density enhancement at the boundary of a uniform dipolar superfluid

## Abstract

> The collective excitations of a quantum liquid lie at the heart of its emergent behaviour. In
> many systems, the variation of excitation energy ${\varepsilon}$ with wavevector $k$ exhibits a
> softening at a characteristic lengthscale, which plays a key role in thermodynamics and
> transport. These excitations, termed rotons, often reflect an underlying tendency of the liquid
> to crystallize into a competing symmetry-broken state, such as the solid state of helium or the
> Wigner crystal of electrons in a magnetic field. More than $50$ years ago, it was predicted that
> rotons should manifest as emergent density oscillations at the fluid's boundary, but this effect
> has not been observed. Here, we realize an ultracold dipolar superfluid confined in a
> homogeneous trapping potential and directly confirm the emergence of rotonic density enhancement
> at its edge. This is accompanied by rotonic density fluctuations across the bulk, which are
> linked to the static edge structure via the fluctuation-dissipation theorem. Increasing the
> dipolar character leads to an azimuthal density modulation of the fluid's boundary, which we
> show to be analogous to the behaviour of a quasi-one-dimensional fluid confined in an annular
> optical potential.
