# Quantitative measurement of microwave power at millikelvin temperatures: Comparison between dispersively and directly coupled superconducting qubits

## Abstract

> The Rabi frequency of a two-level system is directly proportional to the electric field
> amplitude being incident on this system. Taking advantage of this relationship, it is possible
> to use two-level systems as quantitative sensors of electric fields. Here, we apply this concept
> to superconducting qubits and use them for quantitative measurement of microwave power at
> millikelvin temperatures. Our superconducting circuit design includes three transmon qubits
> directly coupled to the central microwave feedline and three transmon qubits coupled
> dispersively via readout resonators to the feedline. We compare both coupling schemes to assess
> their reliability for microwave power sensing. Further, we describe the modeling behind each
> power-sensing method, including an uncertainty estimation based upon repeated measurements. By
> combining dispersively coupled and directly coupled qubits on the same device, we are able to
> explore different power ranges: from $-155\;$dBm to $-130\;$dBm for the directly coupled qubits
> and from $-95\;$dBm to $-55\;$dBm for the dispersively coupled qubits. When determining the
> microwave-line attenuation for a frequency of $6.0\;$GHz from room temperature to the
> millikelvin stage of the cryostat, we obtain best estimates and uncertainties for the directly
> coupled transmons of $(74.7\pm 0.2) \;$dB and for the dispersively coupled transmons of
> $(75.4\pm 6.8) \;$dB. The larger uncertainty of the dispersively coupled qubit measurements
> indicates that these qubits are less reliable for power sensing than the directly coupled
> qubits, most likely, due to the additional coupling resonator, whose transfer function is
> difficult to estimate accurately. Our results constitute the first step towards validation of
> qubit power sensing in dilution fridges, leading towards traceability for cryogenic microwave
> measurements.
