# Bad Diagnosis: Analyzing Consent and Data Collection Practices of Mobile Medical Apps

## Abstract

> Mobile health (mHealth) apps collect personally-identifiable information while handling uniquely
> sensitive medical data, ranging from mental health records to real-time glucose or heart rate
> monitoring -- yet they remain poorly understood at scale. We present the first comprehensive
> analysis of consent and data collection practices across 10,000+ mHealth apps on the Google Play
> Store, combining static analysis and privacy policy examination. We develop a rigorous
> operational definition to distinguish health apps from general wellness programs, and design a
> measurement framework to detect misuse of permissions and violations of consent. Our analyses
> reveal a troubling ecosystem: 69.15% of apps request at least one permission unrelated to their
> stated purpose, with over 52.9% apps requesting location data, and 50.5% and 34.9% apps
> requesting camera and microphone access, respectively -- despite no functional justification
> based on app descriptions and stated purposes. Most alarmingly, nearly 93% of apps fail to
> provide users with a consent form, directly violating regulatory requirements such as GDPR; and
> even among the remaining 7%, 1 in 14 demonstrate a gap between the obtained consent and
> collection of personally identifiable information. These findings demonstrate an urgent need for
> stronger enforcement of privacy regulations in mHealth apps. We plan to release our dataset and
> measurement tools for continued monitoring of this critical space.
