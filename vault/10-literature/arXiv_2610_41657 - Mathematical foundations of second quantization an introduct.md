# Mathematical foundations of second quantization: an introduction through spin-boson models

## Abstract

> These lecture notes originate from a one-semester course on the mathematical foundations of
> second quantization, first given at the Department of Physics of the Friedrich-Alexander-
> Universität Erlangen-Nürnberg in the Winter Semester 2025/26 to a mixed audience of physics and
> mathematics students. Their guiding thread is a class of physical models: spin-boson models,
> describing a two-level system coupled to a boson field. After summarizing the necessary
> background on unbounded operators, up to the Kato-Rellich and KLMN theorems, and developing
> direct sums and tensor products of Hilbert spaces and of unbounded operators, we treat single-
> mode boson fields, constructing the ladder and number operators and characterizing their
> domains, before moving to Fock spaces, second quantization, bosonic and fermionic annihilation
> and creation operators, and the canonical (anti)commutation relations. Throughout, spin-boson
> models provide both the motivation and the test bench for the formalism: we rigorously establish
> their self-adjointness, solve the pure dephasing and Jaynes-Cummings models explicitly, deriving
> their spectra and the qubit channels they induce, show how their multimode counterparts display
> irreversible decoherence and decay, and, in an appendix, prove the rotating-wave approximation
> for the quantum Rabi model with an explicit error bound. Finally, we use the KLMN theorem to
> construct spin-boson models with non-normalizable form factors, and briefly discuss stronger
> ultraviolet divergences and the need for renormalization. Exercises with full solutions are
> included.
