# A Concise Introduction to Quantum Electromagnetics

## Abstract

> This book introduces quantum electromagnetics to readers trained in classical electromagnetics,
> without assuming a systematic background in quantum mechanics. The central question is when
> classical descriptions suffice, and when the quantum states of matter or the field must be
> retained explicitly. Starting from familiar electromagnetic modes and material response, the
> discussion develops atomic dynamics, field quantization, and the interaction of quantized fields
> with quantum matter. Particular attention is given to how mode profiles, Green functions, and
> scattering matrices obtained from classical electromagnetic calculations enter quantum models.
> Atomic coherence, photon statistics, spontaneous emission, and cavity coupling are developed
> through derivations that identify the physical assumptions and limits of each description.
> Examples drawn from atomic clocks, Rydberg-atom receivers, and single-photon devices connect
> these concepts to excitation, propagation, and measurement in practical systems. Loss and noise
> are included in relating internal dynamics to measured signals and correlations. The final
> discussion examines how sensitivity and claims of quantum advantage depend on bandwidth,
> measurement resources, and the classical reference used for comparison. The aim is to help
> readers choose an appropriate model, carry out the calculation, and understand what its
> predictions mean for an electromagnetic measurement.
