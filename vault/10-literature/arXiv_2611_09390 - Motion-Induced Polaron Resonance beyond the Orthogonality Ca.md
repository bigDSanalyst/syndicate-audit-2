# Motion-Induced Polaron Resonance beyond the Orthogonality Catastrophe

## Abstract

> We investigate the spectroscopic response of a repulsively interacting, infinitely heavy
> impurity moving at constant velocity $v$ through a one-dimensional ideal Fermi gas. Using an
> exact functional-determinant approach, we calculate the Ramsey response and spectral function,
> fully accounting for many-body particle-hole excitations. We uncover a strongly nonmonotonic
> dependence on impurity velocity: decoherence is enhanced around the Fermi velocity, whereas
> sufficiently rapid motion restores a narrow, long-lived polaron resonance. A long-time wave-
> packet analysis yields analytical expressions for the polaron energy and decay rate over the
> full velocity range and quantitatively captures the numerical results. At high velocity, the
> polaron energy approaches the mean-field value, the decay rate vanishes as $1/v$, and the
> spectral weight approaches unity. Thus, rapid prescribed motion provides a dynamical route to
> suppressing the orthogonality catastrophe and restoring polaron coherence even for an infinitely
> heavy impurity, where recoil is entirely absent.
