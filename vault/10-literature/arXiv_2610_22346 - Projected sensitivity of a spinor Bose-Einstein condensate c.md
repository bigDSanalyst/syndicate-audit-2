# Projected sensitivity of a spinor Bose-Einstein condensate comagnetometer to proton monopole-dipole interactions

## Abstract

> Light spin-zero bosons with scalar and pseudoscalar couplings mediate a parity- and time-
> reversal-violating monopole-dipole interaction between unpolarized matter and polarized spins.
> For proton spins, the interaction has been probed directly only at ranges $λ\gtrsim$ 5 mm; below
> this range, laboratory limits are inferred from neutron-dominated nuclei through nuclear-spin
> models. We analyze a direct search with a single-domain $^{87}$Rb spinor Bose-Einstein
> condensate in close proximity to a source mass. The F=1 and F=2 manifolds act as two co-located
> magnetometers that precess in opposite directions in a magnetic field but in the same direction
> under the exotic interaction. They thus form a natural comagnetometer, in which the sum of their
> precession angles suppresses field noise by a measured 44 dB and a weighted sum cancels the
> field altogether. Taking as a starting point the demonstrated magnetometry [Palacios et al.
> Proc. Nat. Acad. Sci. \textbf{119}, e2115339119 (2022)] and comagnetometry [Gomez et al. Phys.
> Ref. Lett. \textbf{124}, 170401 (2020)] performance of the $^{87}$Rb single-domain spinor
> condensate at ICFO [Palacios et al. New J. Phys. \textbf{20}, 053008 (2018)], we study the
> projected sensitivity as a function of source-mass geometry and in-progress improvements to atom
> loading methods, trap beam geometry, and magnetic environment. With an insulating bismuth
> germanate source mass with a spherical top, whose apex sits 50 $μ$m below the center of the
> condensate, one week of acquisition would reach $|g_s^N g_p^p|\approx 2\times10^{-22}$ with
> these upgrades and $3\times10^{-24}$ at the projection-noise limit ($λ=100\, μ$m, 95\% C.L.).
> This is more than three and five orders of magnitude below existing laboratory limits. The
> improvement covers boson masses of 0.3-50 meV/c$^2$ after the upgrades and 0.09-70 meV/c$^2$ at
> the projection-noise limit.
