# Surface charges and quantum reflection of hydrogen-isotope beams at grazing incidence

## Abstract

> Experiments with cryogenic beams of hydrogen isotopes (hydrogen (H), deuterium (D), and tritium
> (T)) can be used to study quantum reflection from solid dielectric mirrors. Most dielectric
> surfaces carry trapped electric charges whose field polarizes the incident atom. We study
> theoretically how such charges, distributed randomly, in clusters, with compensation over a
> finite length, or in order, change specular reflection and diffuse scattering of H, D, and T at
> grazing incidence on typical dielectric mirrors (SiO$_2$/Si and MgF$_2$), for longitudinal
> velocities of 100--300~m/s and normal velocities of 3--100~cm/s which might become available
> soon in grazing incidence experiments. Starting from the exact coupled-channel equations, we
> show that the specular wave feels only the laterally averaged polarization potential, which is
> fixed by the structure factor of the charges and, for uncorrelated charges, is an inverse-square
> tail $-C_2/z^2$. The exact solution replaces the linear threshold law $1-\Rr\propto\vperp$ by
> the fractional power $\vperp^{2ν}$, $ν=(1/4-ζ)^{1/2}$, with the coupling $ζ\propto C_2$; for
> weak coupling, this appears as a logarithmic velocity dependence, and for $ζ>1/4$, the
> reflection coefficient oscillates log-periodically. At the surface charge density of $10^{12}$
> cm$^{-2}$, absorption increases by up to 26\% for SiO$_2$/Si and by a factor of two to three for
> MgF$_2$; the effect increases with atomic mass, saturates for compensated charges, and is
> accompanied by diffuse scattering below $10^{-3}$ of charge-induced loss. We also formulate the
> condition of complete absorption at the solid surface.
