# Adding Temperature to the Adiabatic Connection: Hybrid Functionals for Static and Dynamic Correlation

## Abstract

> Capturing static and dynamic electron correlation simultaneously at manageable computational
> cost remains a key challenge in electronic structure theory. Motivated by the recurring role of
> effective temperature in self-consistent field methods we extend the adiabatic connection
> mechanism from density functional theory to include a temperature dimension. This gives rise to
> thermally enhanced hybrid functionals, in which static correlation is captured via fractional
> occupations generated by an effective "correlation temperature" within thermal Hartree-Fock,
> while dynamic correlation is described by established density functional approximations. The
> framework formally unifies information-density matrix functional theory (i-DMFT) and thermally-
> assisted-occupation (TAO-)DFT as special cases, clarifying their theoretical basis and the
> respective prescriptions for the temperature parameter. We assess the performance of several
> thermally enhanced hybrids for different cases where i-DMFT was previously shown to fail.
> Furthermore, we discuss and test different schemes for determining the correlation temperature
> and investigate the accuracy of different such choices for a benchmark set derived from
> biphenyl. The results help to clarify the achievable balance of accuracy, generality, and
> efficiency, establishing thermally enhanced hybrids as a systematic route toward low-cost
> treatment of both correlation types.
