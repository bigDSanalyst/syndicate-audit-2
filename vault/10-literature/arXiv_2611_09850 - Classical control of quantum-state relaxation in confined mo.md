# Classical control of quantum-state relaxation in confined molecular hydrogen

## Abstract

> Quantum-state conversion between the ortho and para nuclear-spin states of H$_2$ is forbidden in
> the isolated molecule but these states can become connected through interactions with a
> surrounding environment. How that same environment governs the dynamics of the newly connected
> states remains poorly understood. Here, we investigate this problem using H$_2$ confined in
> crystalline molecular solids, where the host environment selectively enables conversion between
> a pair of otherwise disconnected ortho-H$_2$ and para-H$_2$ states. Rotationally resolved
> infrared spectroscopy directly determines the state-specific conversion dynamics in crystalline
> CO$_2$ and N$_2$O at 10 K, revealing that increasing H$_2$ concentration suppresses nuclear-spin
> conversion by nearly an order of magnitude, reaching complete suppression by 20% H$_2$ loading.
> Molecular dynamics and Monte Carlo simulations identify two complementary origins of this
> concentration dependence: changes in the thermally populated phonon modes available to mediate
> relaxation, and strain-driven H$_2$ clustering that progressively removes molecules from the
> host crystal field responsible for enabling conversion. The resulting picture connects a
> classical collective response of the host to microscopic quantum-state dynamics: H$_2$ loading
> generates strain that reorganizes the local molecular environment, thereby modifying both the
> phonon bath and the molecular coupling to that bath. These results establish a multiscale
> mechanism by which condensed-phase environments can govern the connectivity and dynamics of an
> effective two-level system.
