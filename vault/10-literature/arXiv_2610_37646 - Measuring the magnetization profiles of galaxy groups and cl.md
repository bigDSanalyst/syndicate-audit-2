# Measuring the magnetization profiles of galaxy groups and clusters

## Abstract

> Magnetic fields are vital in determining the physical conditions of baryons in massive halos.
> They are, however, notoriously difficult to observe. This impacts problems from our
> understanding of galaxy formation, to the observed deficit in the amplitude of thermal Sunyaev-
> Zel'dovich (tSZ) power spectrum that affects cosmological inferences. Although Faraday rotation
> measures (RMs) offer a direct probe of magnetic fields, it weights magnetic fields by the free-
> electron density. Several probes of free electrons, including fast radio burst (FRB) dispersion
> measures (DMs) and kinematic SZ (kSZ) effect, are now maturing into precision tools. We combine
> RM$^2$ stacks from SPICE-RACS around DESI galaxy groups and ROSAT clusters with optical depth
> profiles constrained jointly by the FRB DM-redshift relation and the kSZ effect, and obtain 95%
> upper limits on the rms line-of-sight magnetic field of $<3.9$-$11.6~μ$G ($<2.3$-$6.4~μ$G) in
> groups (clusters) for a 10~kpc coherence length. Combining these with thermal pressure profiles
> inferred from tSZ-shear cross-correlations, we obtain 95% lower limits on the plasma $β$ of
> $>0.31$ ($>11$) in groups (clusters). Although current constraints are only limits, we forecast
> a first detection with larger datasets from the Deep Synoptic Array (DSA). We present the first
> forecast of the DSA RM grid density ($\sim365$ RMs deg$^{-2}$), which when combined with 10$^4$
> FRBs would measure the field in groups to $3$-$13\%$ precision, compared to current constraints
> of $38$-$75\%$. Although the interpretation of current measurements remains challenging owing to
> depolarization along turbulent halo sightlines and the unknown coherence length, larger samples
> and DSA/Square Kilometer Array observations may resolve these uncertainties by measuring
> polarization-fraction profiles and the RM structure function using extended radio sources,
> respectively.
