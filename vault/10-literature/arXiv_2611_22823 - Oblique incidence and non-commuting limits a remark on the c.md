# Oblique incidence and non-commuting limits: a remark on the convergence of Fresnel integrals

## Abstract

> The Gaussian integrals of quantum mechanics and of paraxial optics are oscillatory and only
> conditionally convergent. Textbooks usually make them converge by adding a small imaginary part
> to the time or by analytic continuation. We point out that the one-dimensional Fresnel integral
> converges by itself, while in two dimensions the result depends on the shape of the expanding
> domain: over squares it converges, over discs it oscillates for ever. The second case is the
> field of a uniformly excited plane of dipoles, which Feynman uses to explain the refractive
> index. Lai, Lau and Wong showed that at oblique incidence the integral converges and that normal
> incidence is recovered in the limit; we note that the two limits do not commute, and that this
> provides a transparent introduction to the regularization of the time-sliced path integral,
> where the ordering of the integrations in time plays the role of the physical prescription.
