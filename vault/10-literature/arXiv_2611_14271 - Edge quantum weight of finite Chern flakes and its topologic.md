# Edge quantum weight of finite Chern flakes and its topological floor

## Abstract

> The zero-point energy of a circuit measures the polarization fluctuation of an insulator coupled
> to it. In a crystal this is the bulk quantum weight $K_\mathrm{bulk}$, which the Chern number
> $C$ bounds below by $|C|$. We show that a finite Chern flake adds an edge term that survives as
> the flake grows. The low-frequency transitions of its chiral edge carry a dipole weight, the sum
> of their squared dipole matrix elements, $K_\mathrm{edge}\geq|C|$ per unit area, a floor that
> follows from the positive absorption of both circular polarizations by every transition and the
> known cancellation of circular dichroism between edge and bulk. A weakly coupled circuit slower
> than the edge modes therefore reads $K_\mathrm{bulk}+K_\mathrm{edge}$, averaged over two field
> directions, while a faster one below the bulk gap approaches $K_\mathrm{bulk}$, so which quantum
> weight is measured depends on whether the flake is made large or the circuit slow first. Exact
> free-fermion calculations on lattice flakes bear out the floor and this dependence on the
> circuit frequency. The result holds at weak coupling, which a growing flake at fixed coupling
> eventually leaves. Above the floor, the edge weight of one channel is a number fixed by the
> shape of the boundary and the edge velocities, which square and rectangular lattice flakes
> follow to within 0.003. Each further edge channel adds its own such number, while the circular
> dichroism of the edge counts only the net chirality.
