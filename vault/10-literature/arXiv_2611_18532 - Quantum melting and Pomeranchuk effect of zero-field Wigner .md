# Quantum melting and Pomeranchuk effect of zero-field Wigner solids

## Abstract

> Wigner solids and crystals are many-body phases of matter in which repulsive interactions
> between the charge carriers cause them to freeze into a solid. How Wigner solids compete or
> cooperate with integer and fractional quantum Hall liquids has been a long-standing open
> question. Here, we investigate this question for zero-field, electron and hole Wigner solids in
> cadmium arsenide quantum wells. In the low temperature limit, our results are consistent with a
> recent theoretical model [A. P. Reddy and L. Fu, Phys. Rev. B 113, L161403 (2026)] that proposes
> that the quantum Hall liquid can become energetically favored over the solid at low integer
> filling factors. Going beyond the low-temperature limit, we observe a re-entrant solid that
> emerges from the quantum Hall liquid. We explain this observation by considering the role of
> spin entropy, in close analogy with the zero-field Pomeranchuk effect, but appearing here at
> finite fields. The competition between quantum Hall liquids and Wigner solids is thus governed
> by both energy and entropy terms, giving rise to rich phase stability diagrams that result from
> the interplay between topology and correlations.
