# Squeezed Cooling and Lasing with Cold Atoms in Cavity-QED

## Abstract

> A squeezed laser undergoes a lasing transition into a squeezed cavity mode, where stimulated
> emission generates a bright coherent field with a laser-like coherence time while maintaining
> one field quadrature below the vacuum noise level. Here we propose an optical implementation
> that needs neither a nonlinear crystal nor injected squeezed light, based on engineered
> interactions between ensembles of rubidium atoms and a squeezed cavity mode via cavity-assisted
> Raman transitions. A first ensemble cools the cavity into a squeezed vacuum, a mechanism we term
> squeezed cooling, yielding up to 9 dB in the intracavity field, surpassing the 3 dB limit of
> optical parametric oscillators. Tuned for output instead, it reaches up to 18 dB of output
> squeezing. Incoherently pumping a second ensemble drives a lasing transition into the squeezed
> mode, emitting a bright beam with long coherence time and noise below vacuum spanning a
> megahertz-wide band, away from the sharp phase-diffusion peak of excess noise at the carrier
> frequency, and robust to relaxation oscillations. We characterize lasing thresholds,
> superradiant emission, and linewidth narrowing, and identify the experimentally accessible
> regime where the system exhibits the defining features of a squeezed laser.
