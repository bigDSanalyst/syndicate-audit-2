# A Warped Born Rule Turns an Observer into a Maxwell's Demon

## Abstract

> Quantum mechanics seems to be an incredibly delicate theory. Slight alterations to nearly any
> part of its structure can lead to huge qualitative differences and wildly unphysical
> predictions. Here, we explore the many-body consequences of replacing the Born rule
> $p(m)\propto\|P_m|ψ\rangle|^2$ by a warped rule $p_b(m)\propto\|P_m|ψ\rangle\|^{2b}$.
> Specifically, in systems with conserved particle number, evolution punctuated by repeated local
> particle observations acquires a probabilistic bias over measurement histories with an effect
> controlled by the sign of $b-1$. For $b>1$, this bias generically drives the gas to collect into
> nearly frozen configurations. Similar to the action of Maxwell's demon, the simple act of
> observation seems to extract entropy from a closed system. For $b<1$, the bias is reversed,
> generically scouring out long-wavelength fluctuations to produce a hyperuniform gas. We study
> this effect in a one-dimensional quantum gas, where these two phases are derived analytically
> for a particular measurement scheme and corroborated numerically for more general procedures
> with discrete-time projective-measurement circuit models using matrix product state simulations.
> We additionally discuss how this bias manifests in a common technique that often employs this
> warped rule, the ``replica trick,'' implying qualitative differences between finite-replica and
> replica-limit calculations.
