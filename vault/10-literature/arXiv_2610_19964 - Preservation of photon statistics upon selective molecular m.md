# Preservation of photon statistics upon selective molecular modulation in hollow-core fibers

## Abstract

> Preserving the underlying statistical properties of light during frequency transduction is
> essential for interfacing spectrally disparate quantum systems. To do this successfully, the
> process needs to be highly efficient, broadband and noiseless. Despite significant progress in
> this field, satisfying all these conditions remains challenging. Recently, molecular modulation-
> based transduction in hollow-core fibers has emerged as a promising approach, where biphoton
> correlations were proven to be preserved during frequency up-conversion. However, beyond this
> previous demonstration of correlation-maintaining conversion, whether molecular modulation can
> preserve more general statistical properties of light remains experimentally unexplored. Here,
> we demonstrate the conservation of photon-number statistics during selective frequency
> transduction by Raman molecular modulation in a hydrogen-filled hollow-core fiber. As a proof of
> concept, weak 585-nm signals are selectively converted to 773 nm in the near-infrared and to 471
> nm in the blue spectral region with near-unity efficiencies. Phase matching between the
> generated coherence waves and the transitions of interest is achieved through the effective
> dispersion of the system, enabling independent selection of the frequency up- and down-
> conversion processes. The preservation of the photon-number statistics of the shifted signals,
> along with the large frequency shifts, broad tunability and compatibility with quantum-optical
> fields, provides a versatile fiber-based platform for quantum frequency transduction. In
> particular, the demonstrated interfacing between visible and near-infrared wavelengths using low
> pump energies and low gas pressures offers a route towards connecting spectrally distinct
> quantum systems, including quantum emitters, sensors and atomic quantum memories, within hybrid
> quantum networks.
