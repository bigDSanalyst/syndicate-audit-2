# A Quantum Mechanical Description of Active Matter

## Abstract

> The natural environments habitable to life are inseparable from dissipative and entropic forces,
> which mandate active matter to operate on constant fluxes of energy and momentum to survive and
> propagate through spacetime. In contrast to fundamental particles which are characterized by
> energy and momentum, we postulate that energy expenditure rate and momentum generation rate are
> the fundamental dynamical quantities driving this evolution of swimming active matter and
> accordingly construct the corresponding Lagrangian and Hamiltonian of motion of the active
> matter. Furthermore, living systems broadly employ periodic, non-repeating motion for both
> locomotion and various biological functions, with meaningful information stored in the periods
> of these functions. Consequently, analyzing these dynamics in the Fourier space lands us at the
> familiar tools of the operator based, quantum mechanics. This is accomplished by introducing a
> complex field to describe the evolving probability distribution of a single unit, as well as the
> population density of mutually non-interacting active particles under the constraint of scarce
> resources. The resulting single-unit wavefunction evolves analogously to the Dirac equation with
> minimal coupling, wherein gauge symmetry reflects the generality of the governing equations
> across arbitrary resource landscapes. Additionally, we present a Schrödinger-like equation where
> the effective potential term represents the environmentally mandated, non-motile base metabolic
> rate. Finally, we formalize the bidirectional coupling between active swimmers and the
> surrounding fluid via a multipole expansion of the active force with an emphasis on force
> monopoles and dipoles, where each pole corresponds to a distinct mode of active motility.
