# Adequacy radii for stepped-wedge trials with time-varying effects

## Abstract

> Complete stepped-wedge designs guarantee eventual intervention delivery, but their final periods
> contain no concurrent controls. Under exposure-time heterogeneity this creates a design-analysis
> tension: a constant-effect analysis can be precise while targeting a design-dependent contrast,
> whereas an exposure-time indicator (ETI) analysis targets a prespecified time-averaged effect
> but may be much less precise. We develop an adequacy radius that quantifies the size of
> exposure-time heterogeneity compatible with a prespecified tolerance for estimand mismatch, and
> combine it with precision calculations to compare complete wedges with rollout-constrained
> hybrid designs that retain a wait-list arm. For a balanced wedge with a wait-list under the
> Hussey-Hughes model, the wait-list share and the correlation act on the constant-effect GLS
> estimand through a single scalar; we prove that the radius increases strictly with the wait-list
> share and decreases strictly with the within-cluster correlation, but remains bounded. Numerical
> optimisation and a model-based redesign of a weekend allied-health trial show that intermediate
> hybrids make estimand-aligned ETI analysis materially more affordable, giving a robustness-
> precision frontier rather than a binary choice between a complete wedge and a parallel trial. A
> second trial, of digital adherence support for tuberculosis treatment, has the same radius but
> no detectable heterogeneity; its test of a constant effect would detect heterogeneity large
> enough to halve the coverage of the nominal interval only 37% of the time. The contribution is a
> diagnostic and design principle: when exposure-time heterogeneity is plausible, retaining
> concurrent controls reduces the price of analysing the estimand one intends to report.
