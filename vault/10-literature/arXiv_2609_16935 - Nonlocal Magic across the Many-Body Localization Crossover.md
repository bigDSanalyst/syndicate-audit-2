# Nonlocal Magic across the Many-Body Localization Crossover

## Abstract

> Nonlocal magic quantifies the minimum nonstabilizerness attainable under independent local
> unitary transformations on the two subsystems. Here, we use min-relative nonlocal magic (NLM) to
> characterize the crossover from ergodicity to many-body localization (MBL) in the random-field
> XXZ chain. Unlike entanglement entropy, NLM probes how entanglement is organized through the
> distance of the Schmidt spectrum from dyadic-flat stabilizer spectra. From weak to intermediate
> disorder, NLM evolves from an $O(1)$ Haar-like value into a size-enhanced dome, while
> entanglement remains volume-law, revealing a spectral reorganization not visible in the entropy.
> Deep in the MBL regime, a two-level cut-hybridization model captures the nearly binary Schmidt
> spectrum and explains why the mean and median NLM decay approximately as $W^{-1}$ and $W^{-2}$,
> respectively. Following a product-state quench, NLM overshoots and relaxes in the ergodic
> regime, whereas at strong disorder it grows slowly and approximately logarithmically. These
> results show that NLM resolves Schmidt-spectrum structure not visible in the entanglement
> entropy and provides a complementary probe of ergodicity breaking.
