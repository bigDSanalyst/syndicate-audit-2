# Many-Body Correlations in Phase-Space Microscopes

## Abstract

> The recently proposed "phase-space microscope" (PSM) extends quantum gas microscopes by allowing
> joint, single-shot measurements of position and momentum. We show that the $n$-body correlation
> functions constructed from such phase-space snapshots determine, in principle, all $n$-body
> observables of a many-body quantum state. This establishes phase-space imaging as a complete
> probe of strongly correlated matter, beyond what snapshots of position and momentum can provide
> ---a limitation that goes back to a famous question posed by Pauli. Even without the full
> reconstruction of the density matrices, the PSM provides valuable insight into the quantum
> states. We demonstrate this power through several applications: witnessing entanglement hidden
> from both position and momentum, including between spatially separated collective degrees of
> freedom; resolving pairing structure across the BEC-BCS crossover, including the phase of the
> pair wavefunction and the reconstruction of its two-body Wigner function; probing inhomogeneous
> systems and preformed pairs without long-range order, relevant to the pseudogap phase;
> extracting the interaction energy in a magnetic field; and measurements of the local Chern
> marker. Furthermore, we discuss phase imprinting in the matter-wave Fourier plane as an
> alternative method for probing time-reversal-symmetry-broken phases such as chiral $p$-wave
> superconductors. Together, these results establish the PSM as a versatile new tool for exploring
> fundamental features of quantum many-body phases in strongly correlated quantum matter.
