# OGLE-2016-BLG-0523: A Low-mass Brown Dwarf or super-Jupiter in a $>100$ au Orbit

## Abstract

> To search for very wide-orbit binary-lens events, we cross-match microlensing events detected by
> OGLE-IV and KMTNet between 2011 and 2024. After removing contaminants from variable stars, we
> identify OGLE-2016-BLG-0523 as a microlensing event showing two short-lived bumps in its light
> curve, with the two separated by 8.36 yr. We investigate three scenarios to explain the two
> bumps, including a binary-lens single-source (2L1S), a single-lens binary-source (1L2S), and two
> unrelated single-lens single-source events (2$\times$1L1S). The 1L2S interpretation is excluded
> due to its poorer fit to the data, while the 2$\times$1L1S scenario is disfavored by the low
> event rate, unphysical total source flux, and unphysical color for one of the two putative
> sources. For the surviving 2L1S interpretation, the Einstein timescales of the two bumps are
> $8.343 \pm 0.076$ days and $3.231 \pm 0.047$ days, respectively, implying a binary mass ratio of
> $q = 0.15$. A Bayesian analysis indicates that the primary lens is most likely a low-mass M
> dwarf, while the companion is probably a low-mass brown dwarf or super-Jupiter. The inferred
> projected separation is about 450 au, making the companion the widest low-mass object discovered
> via microlensing to date. Our discovery highlights both the promise and challenges of
> identifying very wide, low-mass companions through microlensing. Although upcoming facilities
> such as Roman and Earth 2.0 space telescopes will improve sensitivity to such events, overcoming
> degeneracies and distinguishing them from variable phenomena will remain essential for building
> a robust statistical sample.
