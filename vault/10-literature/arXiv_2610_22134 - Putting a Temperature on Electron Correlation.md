# Putting a Temperature on Electron Correlation

## Abstract

> It is common wisdom that a better understanding of how to quantify electron correlation should
> lead to more efficient treatments of the correlation problem. Here, we not only substantiate
> this view but turn it into a concrete, systematic framework for ground states. Since prominent
> correlation measures such as the nonfreeness depend only on the one-particle reduced density
> matrix, modeling the correlation energy as a function of such a measure naturally places the
> framework within reduced density matrix functional theory, while keeping the cost at mean-field
> level. Strikingly, the variable conjugate to the measure emerges as a ``correlation
> temperature'', thereby connecting correlation measures, functional theory, and effective
> temperatures. The framework rationalizes methods such as i-DMFT, and we illustrate its key
> concepts analytically for the Hubbard dimer and numerically for small molecular systems. This
> brings a fundamental question within reach: how much of the correlation problem can be solved at
> mean-field cost?
