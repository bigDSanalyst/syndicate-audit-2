# Anomalies and the Berry Phase: From $π^0\toγγ$ to the geometry of the fermion vacuum

## Abstract

> We review the chiral anomaly from a historical perspective, from the $π^0\toγγ$ puzzle and the
> work of Adler, Bell and Jackiw, to the Wess-Zumino-Witten (WZW) term. We then describe how the
> WZW term was motivated from quantum chromodynamics in 1983-85. Integrating out the non-Abelian
> gauge fields, that have a gapped spectrum, gives a Nambu-Jona-Lasinio type theory of quarks, and
> integrating out the quarks, which are gapped by chiral symmetry breaking, gives the WZW model.
> The fermion determinant splits into a regulator-dependent modulus, which gives the dynamics of
> mesons, and a universal phase, which is fixed by the anomaly and is the WZW term. Restricted to
> the collective coordinates of the baryon soliton, the WZW term imposes a U(1) constraint that
> makes the baryon wave functions monopole harmonics and fixes their spin, statistics and flavour
> multiplets. We then explain that this phase is a Berry phase: the Schwinger terms of current
> algebra are the curvature of a U(1) connection on the space of chiral field configurations, and
> the WZW term is its holonomy, which we call a Berry-Wilson loop. In 1+1 dimensions we write the
> connection explicitly in terms of Euler angles, exhibiting a Dirac monopole whose quantised
> charge is the level of the current algebra.
