# Preparing for the Gaia DR4 Epochalypse: Simulated Epoch Astrometry for Substellar Companion Orbit-Modeling Benchmarks

## Abstract

> Gaia's fourth data release (DR4) will provide epoch astrometry for billions of sources, enabling
> the detection of potentially tens of thousands of substellar companions. We present the
> epochalypse catalog: Gaia DR4-like epoch astrometry for testing orbit-modeling pipelines on a
> common set of injected systems with known parameters. Onto each of the $5{,}040{,}328$ main-
> sequence Gaia stars within $500\,{ \rm pc}$ in the G23H catalog, we inject a zero-, one-, and
> two-companions system, for $15{,}120{,}984$ systems in total. Companion masses and semimajor
> axes are drawn log-uniformly over $1\,M_{ \rm Mars}$--$80\,M_{ \rm Jup}$ and $0.03$--$100\,{ \rm
> au}$, with no occurrence model and no detectability cut, so orbital periods extend well beyond
> the mission baseline and the companion-free population serves as a matched null. We also flag
> sub-populations of one- and two-companions systems whose companions are potentially detectable,
> based on an estimated signal-to-noise ratio that accounts for each star's noise, number of
> transits, and the suppression of signals with periods longer than the baseline. As a
> demonstration, we run the kepmodel astrometric periodogram on every system and find that signal
> detection and period localization decouple beyond the DR4 baseline: long-period companions can
> produce significant astrometric curvature while their periods, and therefore their masses,
> remain poorly constrained. Under any companion occurrence model, this population of systems
> where the orbit is detectable but not characterizable will far outnumber the population with
> fully characterizable orbits. The epochalypse catalog, its full generation pipeline, and a
> modeling tutorial are publicly available.
