# Quantum Optimal Control of Many-Body States in an Antiferromagnetic XXZ Chain with a Single Local Magnetic Field

## Abstract

> We investigate the quantum optimal control of an antiferromagnetic XXZ spin chain with open
> boundary conditions, driven solely by a local time-dependent magnetic field applied to a single
> terminal spin. By optimizing the control field via Krotov's algorithm, we evaluate the
> feasibility of preparing multi-particle many-body states, including highly polarized
> Greenberger-Horne-Zeilinger (GHZ)-type states and study the mechanisms by which the control is
> applied. We demonstrate that global spin-flip symmetry restricts reachable state spaces,
> strictly confining dynamics within the parity sector of the initial ground state and requiring
> parity-adapted linear combinations to avoid fundamental population upper bounds. Beyond symmetry
> constraints, driven population transfer does not occur via direct multi-spin flips, but proceeds
> stepwise across intermediate magnetization sectors ($|S_{\mathrm{tot}}^z| = 0 \to 1 \to 2 \to
> 3$) mediated by resonant frequency components matching the internal energy gaps. To overcome
> optimization stagnation caused by the small overlap mechanism in gradient updates, we introduce
> a four-stage sequential target protocol and compared it to spectrally filtered, time-localized
> frequency seed pulses. Combining frequency-tailored seed fields with an extended propagation
> time window yields GHZ state preparation fidelities exceeding $0.927$ establishing that a single
> local actuator is sufficient for high-fidelity multipartite state engineering in interacting
> spin networks.
