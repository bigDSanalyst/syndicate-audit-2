# Electrostatic Limits on Quantum Spatial Search

## Abstract

> We prove that a quantum walker cannot search a two-dimensional lattice in Grover's $O(\sqrt N)$
> time, resolving the long-standing open question posed by Aaronson and Ambainis in 2003. A walker
> that hops between neighbouring sites of an $L\times L$ lattice, $N=L^2$, needs $Ω(\sqrt{N\log
> N})$ local steps at least to find a marked vertex, even with unlimited memory and free queries.
> Hence, Tulsi's algorithm is asymptotically optimal. Our proof rests on a surprising
> correspondence with classical electrostatics. We treat the marked vertex as a point charge and
> weight the overlap between the walker's states with and without the mark by the potential drop,
> which vanishes where queries act. Only local steps change this weighted overlap, at a rate
> limited by the field energy, so the lower bound is set by the self-potential of the charge. In
> the plane this self-potential grows logarithmically with system size, like the Coulomb
> potential, leading to the extra root-logarithmic factor. Several marked vertices turn the charge
> into a conductor, and the lower bound depends on their arrangement through its capacitance.
