# Dynamical Casimir emission from Floquet-driven Landau polaritons: Gauge invariance forbids a superradiant phase transition

## Abstract

> Ultrastrong light--matter coupling of linear systems, such as Landau polaritons formed by a two-
> dimensional electron gas in a terahertz cavity, is described by the bosonic Hopfield model, and
> gauge invariance forbids a superradiant phase transition (SRPT). Modulating the magnetic field
> alone has been predicted to enhance the cycle-averaged coupling and induce a Floquet SRPT. We
> develop an exact theory of periodically driven Landau polaritons. Including the electric field
> induced by the modulated flux (Faraday's law), the problem reduces exactly, by Kohn's theorem,
> to the electronic center-of-mass mode coupled to the cavity, whose Hamiltonian is a sum of
> squares at every instant. A theorem of Krein then guarantees stability to all orders above a
> computable drive frequency, the Krein frequency, for which we give a self-contained proof. Below
> it, the system is stable except within resonance tongues where the drive creates pairs from the
> vacuum, the dynamical Casimir effect. At high drive frequency the electrons experience the mean
> field plus a ponderomotive trap (the Fock--Darwin problem), not an enhanced coupling. A gauge
> theorem excludes a Floquet photon condensate for any band structure, including Kane
> nonparabolicity, for which we derive a generalized Thomas--Reiche--Kuhn (TRK) sum rule. The
> predicted transition arises from averaging the coupling in a fixed Landau-level basis, which
> yields an effective model that violates the TRK sum rule; under driving, gauge invariance also
> requires the induced Faraday field. The rule to modulate and average the minimal-coupling
> Hamiltonian, not the parameters of its projection, applies to any linear, minimally coupled
> system. Instead of an SRPT, magnetic modulation produces dynamical Casimir emission: Faraday-
> driven polariton--magnetoplasmon pairs at low threshold, and two-color photon pairs with up to 5
> --10~dB of two-mode squeezing.
