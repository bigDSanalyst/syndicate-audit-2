# Structure of correlations for the single-$j$ model using quantum information theory

## Abstract

> We say that a system undergoes a quantum phase transition when the mean-field ground state of a
> given Hamiltonian spontaneously breaks its symmetries depending on the Hamiltonian parameters.
> It is a widely accepted view that this phenomenon enables the mean-field state to absorb the
> maximum amount of correlations without losing its mean-field structure. However, it is still
> uncertain how those correlations are incorporated within the mean-field state and how the
> correlation structure changes when we get closer to the exact ground state by restoring the
> symmetries. In this work, we answer these questions by studying the single-$j$ model, which is a
> realistic fermionic nuclear model with abelian and non-abelian broken symmetries. We find that
> both types of symmetries have very different effects for the correlations within the mean-field
> state. This makes unclear the concept of the symmetry breaking process as a mechanism to absorb
> the maximum amount of correlations. Moreover, the symmetry restoration redistributes existing
> `fake' correlations between paired orbitals to more complex subsystems.
