# Local Dephasing Sustains Continuous Time Crystals in a Spin-1/2 Ensemble

## Abstract

> Local pure dephasing suppresses single-spin coherence, yet we show that it sustains continuous
> time-crystalline order in a fully connected ferromagnetic spin-$1/2$ ensemble with local decay.
> An exact operator identity reveals the mechanism: dephasing raises the interaction energy, while
> energy relaxation on a finite timescale converts this charging into delayed feedback. Within
> homogeneous mean-field theory, we prove that dephasing is necessary for periodic motion in this
> model and that feedback exactly balances transverse damping over each cycle. Beyond mean field,
> the identity fixes the relative amplitude and phase of energy and longitudinal magnetization in
> oscillatory Liouvillian modes. Liouvillian spectra and stationary two-time correlations
> characterize temporal order through decay rates that decrease algebraically with system size,
> frequencies approaching nonzero mean-field values, and comparable per-spin oscillatory weights.
> Semiclassical trajectories further reveal recurrent switching between a limit-cycle region and
> two symmetry-related stationary regions: fluctuations interrupt and reactivate collective
> oscillations, and oscillatory episodes provide an intermediate channel between stationary
> regions. These results establish local dephasing as a resource for autonomous collective motion
> and show how the resulting temporal order can participate in metastable dynamics.
