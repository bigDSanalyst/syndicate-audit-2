# Exact decoherence dynamics of a detector coupled to vacuum fluctuations in bounded geometries

## Abstract

> The properties of quantum vacuum are highly sensitive to the underlying spacetime geometry and
> boundary conditions. We investigate in this paper the decoherence dynamics of a two-level system
> (detector) coupled to a vacuum scalar field through the $\hatσ_z$-coupling scheme under various
> boundary environments. By rigorously evaluating the reduced density matrix of the detector, we
> derive an exact general expression for the detector's decoherence function, $γ(Δt)$, and use it
> to characterize the dynamical decoherence of a detector in three scenarios: free space, a
> perfectly reflecting boundary, and a cavity formed by two parallel reflecting boundaries. We
> find that in free space the detector's coherence decays monotonically with time, indicating that
> vacuum fluctuations alone inevitably lead to a complete loss of coherence. In the single-
> boundary case, by contrast, the detector's coherence displays non-monotonic time evolution:
> $γ(Δt)$ increases with time up to a maximum and then settles to a steady value, indicating that
> the initial coherence can be partially preserved due to the presence of the boundary. When the
> detector is placed inside a cavity, its coherence evolution exhibits oscillatory behavior, and a
> significant fraction of the initial coherence survives. Moreover, for the same detector-boundary
> distance, more coherence is preserved in the cavity case than in the single-boundary case. These
> results provide a viable route for engineering quantum coherence via boundary conditions.
