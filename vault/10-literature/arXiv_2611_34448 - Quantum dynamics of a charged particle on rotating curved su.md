# Quantum dynamics of a charged particle on rotating curved surfaces and space curves in electromagnetic fields

## Abstract

> We develop a gauge-consistent framework for a charged particle confined to a rotating curved
> manifold in an electromagnetic field, starting from the covariant Klein--Gordon equation. The
> non-relativistic reduction casts the rotational effects as effective gauge potentials while
> preserving the $U(1)$ symmetry, so that rotation and the external field enter alike, the
> geometry entering separately as the da Costa potential. The obstruction to dimensional reduction
> is gauge--geometry entanglement: transverse magnetic fields on a two-dimensional (2D) surface
> and structural torsion on a one-dimensional (1D) curve couple the tangential dynamics to the
> transverse degrees of freedom. We remove this entanglement by three mechanisms---a generalized
> transverse Poincaré gauge, the rotation-minimizing Bishop frame, and the parity of the
> transverse ground state, the last of which annihilates the residual first-order magnetic
> coupling for a single inversion-symmetric well. On a torus, an axial field generates a joint
> Sagnac--Aharonov--Bohm residual of tunable sign without spoiling the poloidal--azimuthal
> separability, whereas an in-plane field couples only through the out-of-plane extent of the
> structure: it breaks the axial symmetry there but leaves a planar curve unaffected to first
> order. The same torus shows the axial field tuning the curvature-induced localization, and a
> corrugated quantum ring converts periodic geometric and flux modulations into an artificial
> superlattice whose in-plane-field immunity follows from planarity and parity, not from the
> smallness of the field. Non-inertial quantum dynamics on surfaces and curves of arbitrary shape
> is thus reduced, within the thin-layer regime, to two ingredients: an effective gauge potential
> and a geometric potential.
