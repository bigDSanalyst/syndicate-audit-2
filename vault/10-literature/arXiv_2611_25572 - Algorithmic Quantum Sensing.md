# Algorithmic Quantum Sensing

## Abstract

> We introduce a unified framework for sensing time-dependent fields through the lens of classical
> and quantum algorithms. First, we develop three pulse sequence protocols, namely randomized,
> matched, and pooled spectroscopy, which outperform established approaches such as dynamical
> decoupling across a wide range of tasks. These protocols attain the optimal sensitivity for
> linear and nonlinear AC sensing on conventional spin or qubit platforms, improving existing
> devices by changing only their pulse sequences. We then introduce quantum computational
> spectroscopy (QCS), which couples quantum sensors to a quantum processor. QCS combines two new
> primitives: a quantum computational frequency comb that coherently converts any classical signal
> into a quantum oracle, and a family of quantum algorithms that use the oracle to extract signal
> information in superposition. Variations can be implemented on current platforms with only one
> memory ancilla. We show that the ultimate limit imposed by quantum mechanics lies polynomially
> beyond the Heisenberg limit, and that QCS achieves it. Attaining this new limit requires quantum
> computation, establishing a fundamental separation between conventional and quantum
> computational sensing. Moreover, QCS retains beyond-Heisenberg scaling for broadband signals
> under constant-strength sensor depolarization, while we prove that the same noise limits any
> protocol lacking quantum computation to standard-quantum-limit scaling. Our result provides the
> first asymptotic, beyond-quadratic quantum advantage in AC metrology when noise is aligned with
> the signal, proving that Lindblad span conditions do not apply to broadband quantum sensing. Our
> work builds an algorithmic theory for designing sensing protocols and establishes quantum
> sensing as a new frontier for quantum algorithms, with quantum advantages that surpass the
> Heisenberg limit and that persist under noise.
