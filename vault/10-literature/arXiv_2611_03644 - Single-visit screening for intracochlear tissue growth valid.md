# Single-visit screening for intracochlear tissue growth validated against multi-visit electrical measurement changes

## Abstract

> Purpose: Tissue growth (TG) around cochlear implant electrodes raises impedance but is not
> reliably visible on CT scans. Routinely recorded stimulus current-induced non-stimulating
> electrode voltages (SCINSEVs) carry this information, but a single recording has no patient-
> specific reference. We present an algorithm for detecting signs of significant TG from a single
> SCINSEV measurement. Methods: TG is assumed to concentrate basally near the electrode insertion
> site, leaving the apical region mostly unaffected. A patient-specific forward model built from
> CT is fitted to the apical measurements and predicts the basal ones. The misfit score, T,
> measures each basal contact's signed relative misfit against the apical misfit, accounting for
> apical fit quality and measurement noise. An ear is flagged when two adjacent basal contacts
> cross a predetermined threshold. We compare T with T-delta, a model-free reference, tracking
> basal relative to apical SCINSEV change since the intra-operative recording, persisting over the
> last two visits. Results: T had a false-positive rate of 3% on growth-free ears. It agreed with
> T-delta in 81% of 68 ears (sensitivity 71%; specificity 86%; p = 6e-6). Over 200 patient-level
> two-fold splits, median held-out agreement was 78% at a false-positive rate of 5.1%. Conclusion:
> An apically fitted patient-specific model predicts basal SCINSEVs well enough to flag signs of
> significant TG at a single visit, without an intra-operative baseline. Detections largely agree
> with persistent multi-visit basal-versus-apical change, a surrogate ground truth for significant
> TG.
