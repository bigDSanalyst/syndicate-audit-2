# Joint analysis of the Macquart relation and the dispersion measure power spectrum: cross-covariance and redshift self-calibration

## Abstract

> The dispersion measures (DMs) of fast radio bursts (FRBs) trace ionised baryons along the line
> of sight, probing baryonic feedback. Two complementary observables can be constructed from an
> FRB catalogue: the DM-redshift (Macquart) relation of host-identified FRBs, and the angular
> power spectrum of the DM field of the full catalogue. Their joint analysis requires their cross-
> covariance. We derive it within the halo model and show it is a super-sample effect: only the
> monopole of an individual DM correlates with the angular power spectrum, yielding an exact,
> model-independent bound on the correlation coefficient. We validate the calculation against log-
> normal forward simulations of $10^4$ FRBs. For individual DMs, the correlation with the
> bandpowers is at the per cent level. Neglecting the cross-covariance changes the width of the
> $χ^2$ distribution by only 0.8 per cent, so the two observables can be treated as effectively
> independent for wide-area surveys. In a joint forecast with an LSST-like weak lensing survey,
> adding the Macquart relation tightens constraints on the physical baryon density by 32 per cent
> and on the mean host contribution by a factor of 4.1, while improving baryonification parameters
> by only about 5 per cent, indirectly through the host contribution. Finally, we show that the
> redshift distribution of FRBs without identified hosts, which enters every DM power spectrum,
> can be reconstructed by inverting their DMs with the jointly fitted mean relation without
> biasing the inferred parameters, whereas a 20 per cent error in the mean relation biases the DM
> power spectrum by up to 2$σ$ per band for $5\times 10^5$ FRBs.
