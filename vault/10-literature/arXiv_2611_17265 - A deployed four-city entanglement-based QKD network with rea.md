# A deployed four-city entanglement-based QKD network with real-time key delivery, benchmarked against finite-key security bounds

## Abstract

> Quantum key distribution (QKD) generates shared secret keys whose security rests on the laws of
> physics rather than on computational assumptions. We report the entanglement-based segment of
> the Slovak national quantum communication infrastructure, connecting four cities in western
> Slovakia over deployed telecommunication fiber. Polarization-entangled photons generated at a
> central node are passively routed by wavelength to form a trusted-node-free, fully meshed sub-
> network of three cities, with the fourth connected through the central node and all links
> operated simultaneously. We also introduce a matrix formalism for passive wavelength routing
> that enables the algorithmic construction of uniquely and fully connected topologies. Unlike
> most previous demonstrations, which estimate key rates from raw coincidences, all rates reported
> here are obtained after the complete post-processing chain, with keys delivered in real time via
> ETSI GS QKD 014. With partial non-local chromatic dispersion compensation, we obtain 13.5 bit/s
> over 109 km, and 4.3 bit/s over 32.1 dB of accumulated loss between two cities without a direct
> fiber link. Benchmarking against finite-key security bounds shows that the implemented heuristic
> privacy amplification can exceed the finite-key bound, and that replacing detect-and-discard
> with error correction would increase the finite-key rate by a factor of 4.1-6.6, and yield a
> positive finite-key rate on the 142.9 km link where the implemented scheme yields none.
