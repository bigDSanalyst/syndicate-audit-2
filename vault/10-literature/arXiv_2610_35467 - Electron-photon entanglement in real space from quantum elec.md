# Electron-photon entanglement in real space from quantum electrodynamics

## Abstract

> We study the entanglement between an electron and the photon it scatters, resolved in real space
> and time. Starting from nonrelativistic QED in Fermi's oscillator representation, we write
> Fock's coupled equations for components with fixed photon number in the photon configuration
> space, in the length gauge, where the photon is created around the atom through a nonlocal
> kernel. Truncated at one photon, the equations are solved numerically without the rotating-wave
> approximation for a one-dimensional model atom in a waveguide with a single transverse mode and
> enhanced coupling. The atom is driven by a short, off-resonant laser pulse, with the electron
> and photon represented on real-space grids. The global electron-photon entanglement is largely
> transient. It peaks during the pulse and decreases as the polarized electron cloud returns to
> the core, leaving a state close to a product of the electron state and a superposition of the
> field vacuum with a single scattered photon. Local entanglement, obtained from walkers sampled
> across both photon-number components, exhibits opposite spatial patterns for the two particles.
> The electron entropy is smallest at the nucleus and maps the local strength of the dipole
> coupling, whereas the photon entropy is largest near the atom, where photons are created and
> absorbed, and smaller in the outgoing wave packets.
