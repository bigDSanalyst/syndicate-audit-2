# Precise experimental investigation of the reflection properties caused by s-polarized evanescent waves and the Casimir puzzle

## Abstract

> We report a high-precision experiment on measuring the low-frequency magnetic field reflected
> from a metallic plate. This experiment is designed to give insight into why theoretical
> predictions of the fundamental Lifshitz theory using the standard response function to the
> electromagnetic waves are in contradiction with the data of numerous precision measurements of
> the Casimir force (the Casimir puzzle). To attain these ends, we measured the magnitude and
> phase of the lateral component of the magnetic field produced by an oscillating magnetic dipole
> and reflected from a metallic plate. These quantities are completely determined by the
> evanescent waves with the transverse electric (s) polarization. The obtained measurement data
> are compared with theoretical predictions using the standard Drude response function of the
> metal to the low-frequency electromagnetic waves. It is shown that there are both quantitative
> and qualitative disagreements with these predictions, so that in the region of low-frequency
> s-polarized evanescent waves the Drude response function is excluded by the data. Keeping in
> mind that just the s-polarized evanescent waves are responsible for the Casimir puzzle, our
> results highlight the causes for its emergence. It is emphasized that, in addition to the
> Casimir effect, searching for the adequate nonlocal response functions of metals to the
> evanescent waves is of utmost significance for many areas of physics as near-field optical
> microscopy, nanophotonics, surface plasmon polaritons, radiative heat transfer, and others.
