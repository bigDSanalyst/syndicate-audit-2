# No free lattice contains an infinite ascending chain of copies of FL(3)

## Abstract

> No free lattice contains an infinite strictly ascending chain of sublattices isomorphic to
> FL(3), the free lattice on three generators; this answers a question that R. A. Dean asked and
> the second author's 1997 notes on the problem left open. Every such chain would yield, inside
> FL(3), a coherent chain of proper self-embeddings $Θ_0, Θ_1, Θ_2, \dots$ with $Θ_k \circ Θ_j =
> Θ_j$ for $j < k$, so that each later embedding fixes the image of each earlier one pointwise.
> Every proper self-embedding of FL(3) whose fixed sublattice contains a free generating triple
> fixes one of the three generators, say $z$, because a power of it or of its dual lies above the
> identity, and a self-embedding that moves every generator and lies above the identity fixes at
> most eleven elements. Up to duality, a proper self-embedding fixing the image of such a $Θ_1$
> pointwise fixes only elements above $z \wedge (x \vee y)$, where no free generating triple
> containing $z$ can lie. We also verify the one-step example of the 1997 notes, prove that no
> step toward such a chain is self-similar, and show that the configuration the argument rules out
> at length three does occur at length two. Without freeness the answer reverses: FL(3) contains
> an infinite strictly ascending chain of 3-generated sublattices, so the lattice analogue of
> Takahasi's chain condition for free groups fails. The theorem itself is checked by the Agda
> proof assistant under its safe flags, for free lattices over generators with decidable equality,
> and its formal proof uses no classical principle: finite searches and an induction on a finite
> set replace the infinite pigeonhole principle by which a chain yields a coherent chain.
