# Non-compact Kogut-Susskind gauge theories: 1D lattice models with exact ${\rm SL}(2,\mathbb{R})$ gauge invariance

## Abstract

> The Kogut-Susskind (KS) formulation of gauge theories constructs quantum many-body Hamiltonians
> with gauge invariance under a compact Lie group exactly implemented on a spatial lattice. Here,
> we revisit this construction for a non-compact gauge group. Although motivated in part by
> theories like general relativity whose first-order formulation involves the non-compact Lorentz
> group as a local symmetry, we focus on simpler cases in one spatial dimension akin to the
> Schwinger model. In a bottom-up approach, we show how exact gauge invariance under the simplest
> non-compact Lie group ${\rm SL}(2,\mathbb{R})$ can be retained exactly on a 1D lattice, and
> highlight the differences that arise with respect to compact gauge groups, including a
> continuous spectrum of the Casimir operators of the gauge fields and the emergence of infinite-
> dimensional representations of the matter fields. For the pure gauge theory, we discuss how a
> consistent restriction to the positive semigroup ${\rm SL}^{+}(2,\mathbb{R})$ reproduces well-
> known results from the quantization of Jackiw-Teitelboim (JT) gravity. Motivated by the
> constraint structure of general dilaton gravity, we introduce static charges coupled to the non-
> compact gauge field, which can be realised by single-fermion degrees of freedom living in an
> additional, synthetic dimension. With static charges our lattice model remains exactly
> integrable, but when extending to dynamical fermionic matter, we arrive at an interacting 1D
> ${\rm SL}(2,\mathbb{R})$ KS gauge theory. We give an interpretation of this model as a chain of
> charges sequentially connected by strings whose tension pulls matter left or right depending on
> the sign of the relative continuous ${\rm SL}(2,\mathbb{R})$ fluxes. This picture becomes exact
> when the involved ${\rm SL}(2,\mathbb{R})$ charges are strongly self-interacting, where we solve
> the reduced two-body problem analytically.
