# When Does Storage Complement Solar? Investment and Cost Recovery under Advanced Net Metering

## Abstract

> We examine the joint PV and storage operational and investment decisions of a prosumer under net
> energy metering (NEM) and peak/off-peak tariffs. Legacy NEM compensates net exports at the
> retail rate over a netting period, typically under time-invariant pricing. To better reflect
> system costs, tariff features are being reconsidered, with many U.S. states adopting time-
> varying prices and export compensation below the retail price. We formulate the prosumer
> decision problem as a cost minimization problem with charge/discharge, PV investment, and
> storage investment decisions. We analytically derive the optimal decisions based on the tariff's
> membership in one of six regions that partition the tariff space. Using these solutions, we
> examine the relationship between PV and storage, identifying when they are complements or
> substitutes. Furthermore, we identify the relationship between private DER incentives and the
> distribution utility's (DU's) net cost of serving the prosumer. For both storage and PV, when
> investment costs are greater than the costs of serving the customer, there is no tariff where
> investment in PV and storage decrease non-adopter bills. Under the same conditions,
> complementarity between PV and storage decreases DU cost recovery for a given tariff. In a
> Massachusetts-based case study, we validate our theoretical findings by connecting tariff design
> features to DER interaction, the DU's net cost, and DER adoption in a stochastic, multi-
> prosumer, and multi-period setting.
