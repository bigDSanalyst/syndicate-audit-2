# Nonperturbative string dynamics in a (2+1)D lattice gauge theory on a superconducting quantum processor

## Abstract

> Electric flux strings underpin confinement in gauge theories. In two spatial dimensions, their
> evolution involves competing processes of charge motion, pair creation, and transverse
> deformation. Accessing these dynamics beyond perturbative limits challenges classical
> approaches, as entanglement growth restricts the times accessible to tensor-network simulations.
> Here we use quantum simulation to investigate a (2+1)D Z2 lattice gauge theory with dynamical
> matter and an explicit magnetic plaquette interaction, spanning strongly confined and
> nonperturbative deconfined regimes. Using 63 superconducting qubits, including 12 ancillas, we
> evolve initially localized strings with static or dynamical endpoint charges. Spatially resolved
> measurements reveal rapid charge and flux spreading in the deconfined regime, enhanced matter
> occupation in the string interior near the pair-creation resonance, and suppressed spreading at
> strong electric fields. String-path occupations further probe transverse rearrangements between
> configurations connected by plaquette operations. Retaining separate matter and gauge degrees of
> freedom makes local Gauss-law constraints directly accessible for error detection. Combining
> these constraints with ancilla checks and noise rescaling, we measure dynamics in circuits
> containing up to 15 second-order Trotter steps. Our results establish quantum simulation as a
> practical approach to exploring nonperturbative string dynamics in two spatial dimensions, with
> gauge redundancy serving as an experimental resource.
