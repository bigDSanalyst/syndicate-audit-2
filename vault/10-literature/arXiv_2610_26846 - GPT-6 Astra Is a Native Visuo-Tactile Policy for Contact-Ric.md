# GPT-6 Astra Is a Native Visuo-Tactile Policy for Contact-Rich Manipulation

## Abstract

> Recent advances in multimodal models have enabled GPT-6 Astra to perform robotic manipulation
> through in-context learning. Yet contact-rich control and tactile perception are often regarded
> as strengths of specialist policies, leaving open whether Astra can directly understand and use
> tactile observations. We provide, to our knowledge, the first demonstration that Astra directly
> interprets vision-based tactile images and uses their contact cues for manipulation. Tactile RGB
> images enter through its existing visual interface, enabling few-shot closed-loop control on
> nineteen simulated UniVTAC and OpenViTac tasks with 210 sampled scene seeds per setting. With
> visuo-tactile input, Astra achieves mean success rates of 93.8% and 81.8%, exceeding the
> strongest reported specialist baselines in our comparison by 22.9 and 13.1 percentage points,
> respectively. Vision-only performance also leads these baselines, reaching 76.3% and 80.6%.
> Trajectory analysis reveals active probing, cautious grasping, contact-guided alignment, and
> recovery, alongside difficulties with tangential loading and corrective execution. Our findings
> challenge the exclusivity of specialist policies in contact-rich manipulation and show that
> fine-grained vision can partially compensate for missing touch. They also identify vision-based
> tactile sensing as an interface for scaling contact understanding with foundation models and
> reveal signs of benchmark saturation. These results motivate joint visual-tactile pretraining,
> harder contact-rich evaluations, and physical-robot validation.
