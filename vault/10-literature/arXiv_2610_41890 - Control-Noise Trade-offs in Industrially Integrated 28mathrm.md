# Control-Noise Trade-offs in Industrially Integrated ${}^{28}\mathrm{Si}/\mathrm{SiGe}$ Spin-1/2 Qubits

## Abstract

> Scaling silicon spin qubits to fault-tolerant processors requires semiconductor foundry
> integration, yet it remains unclear how industrial fabrication and dense device integration
> affect qubit control and noise. Here we study ${}^{28}\mathrm{Si}/\mathrm{SiGe}$ electron
> spin-1/2 qubits fabricated using Intel's industry-compatible 300 mm process and incorporating
> micromagnets for electrically driven spin resonance, using a high-performance device fabricated
> in the RIKEN research cleanroom as a reference. Primitive $π/2$-gate fidelities reach the 99.99%
> level in the Intel devices, which also exhibit longer idle coherence, weaker low-frequency
> correlations and reduced qubit-to-qubit variability across several measured characteristics. In
> the present Intel devices, the micromagnets are integrated inline with the back-end-of-line
> (BEOL) contact routing, with their separation from the quantum well set through the vertical
> stack design. Relative to the reference, their larger separation contributes to weaker
> longitudinal and transverse magnetic-field gradients. The weaker longitudinal gradient
> suppresses the conversion of charge noise into qubit-frequency fluctuations, whereas the weaker
> transverse gradient reduces electrically driven spin-resonance efficiency and makes drive-
> dependent errors relevant at lower Rabi frequencies. Additional BEOL routing and metal levels
> required for two-dimensional arrays make such placement decisions increasingly consequential
> when scaling. These results establish an integration-level control-noise trade-off linking
> micromagnet placement within densely routed layouts to achievable gate performance, while very
> high-fidelity operation remains possible in industrially fabricated devices.
