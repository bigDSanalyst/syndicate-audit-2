# Dephasing-Powered Autonomous Quantum Engine with Time-Crystalline Work Cycles

## Abstract

> Autonomous quantum engines seek to convert energy supplied by environments into useful work, yet
> dephasing is usually regarded as detrimental noise. Here we show that local dephasing can
> instead power a quantum engine: by disrupting ferromagnetic correlations in an interacting
> spin-$1/2$ ensemble, it injects energy that can be transferred through an LC resonator to a
> passive load. Remarkably, without any external periodic driving, the coupled system can self-
> organize into a time-crystalline work cycle that delivers sustained, reproducible power. Within
> homogeneous mean-field dynamics, we identify two distinct routes to such operation. At weak spin
> --resonator coupling, the dissipative time crystal of the unloaded working medium survives
> loading and drives the receiver. At stronger coupling, resonator backaction can disrupt cyclic
> operation, whereas appropriate load damping restores a stable, self-consistent periodic cycle of
> the composite system. Finite-size spin-diffusion simulations further reveal rapidly increasing
> uninterrupted operating times with different size dependences in the two regimes. Our results
> demonstrate how an autonomous quantum engine can self-organize its collective dynamics through
> interactions and load feedback to harness environmental noise for sustained cyclic work output.
