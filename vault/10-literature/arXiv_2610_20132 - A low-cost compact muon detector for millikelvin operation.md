# A low-cost compact muon detector for millikelvin operation

## Abstract

> Ionizing radiation produces correlated errors in superconducting qubits, posing some challenges
> to quantum error correction. In addition, it causes background events in other superconducting
> cryogenic devices, such as kinetic inductance detectors and superconducting nanowire single-
> photon detectors. Cosmic muons are a component of this background that can only be partly
> shielded against at ground level, but can be tagged by an independent detector inside the
> cryostat, so that the affected data can be vetoed, or the muon tags incorporated into error
> correction. We present a low-cost, compact muon detector consisting of two plastic scintillator
> modules, each read out by a silicon photomultiplier (SiPM), operated in coincidence. We
> characterize the SiPMs from room temperature down to 10 mK, including the breakdown and upper-
> limit voltages that define the operating bias range. Operated as a muon counter, the detector
> measures a coincidence rate that, as expected, is independent of temperature within
> uncertainties and agrees with analytical and Monte Carlo predictions. Below approximately 8 K,
> each muon event produces two pulses, one narrow and one broad, an effect that, to our knowledge,
> has not been reported before. Coincidences with an independent muon detector outside the
> cryostat confirm that muons are still counted reliably in this regime. By studying the
> appearance of double pulses as a function of temperature and magnetic field up to 4.5 T, we find
> a behavior consistent with the phase diagram of a type-II superconductor. This links the double
> pulses to a superconducting material in the SiPM, likely an adhesion layer or diffusion barrier
> introduced during manufacturing. The mechanism by which the superconducting state produces the
> second pulse is not yet understood.
