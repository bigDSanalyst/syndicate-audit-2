# Flashing with colours: Photometric indicators of an early interaction in type II supernovae

## Abstract

> We present early-time optical photometry and spectroscopy for a sample 27 previously unpublished
> type II supernovae (SNe II) observed by the Precision Observations of Infant Supernova
> Explosions (POISE) collaboration, combined with a literature sample of 59 events to form a total
> sample of 86 objects. The resulting light curves comprise near-daily cadence, percent-level
> precision $uBgVri$ photometry. From spectra acquired within four days of the estimated explosion
> time, transient narrow emission lines of highly ionised elements, referred to as `flash
> features', can be identified. Based on the presence or absence of these lines, the SNe were
> categorised. We estimate key photometric properties, including rise times, peak absolute
> magnitude, and colour evolution. Our tests indicate that flash SNe are, on average, more
> luminous at the peak than their non-flash counterparts, which is consistent with previous
> studies. Interestingly, statistically significant differences were found in the rise times in
> the $B$ band, with flash SNe exhibiting a tail towards longer rise times. Flash SNe are
> generally bluer than non-flash events, with distinct colour distributions at 20 days post
> explosion, likely reflecting intrinsic differences rather than host-galaxy extinction. Colour-
> curve breaks within 30 days of explosion provide a clear separation between flash and non-flash
> SNe. The change in slopes across the break, combined with the absolute peak magnitude, yield a
> strong diagnostic of the flash-spectroscopic properties. This indicates that photometric
> observations alone may be used to identify SNe experiencing an interaction with dense
> circumstellar material, even in the absence of early-time spectroscopy.
